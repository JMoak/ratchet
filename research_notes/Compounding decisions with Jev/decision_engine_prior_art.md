# Prior art and adjacent landscape for a generic software "decision engine"

Scope: systems that make, gate, route, score, record and learn from many small decisions, surveyed as of 15 October 2026. For each system or category the notes cover what it is, core abstractions and vocabulary, how a decision is represented, how uncertainty/confidence is handled, rollout support (shadow, canary, thresholds), recording of decisions and outcomes, open-source status (license, language), and what to borrow or avoid. A gap analysis closes the document. Everything in "Cited Findings" is sourced; "Inferences" are my synthesis; "Gaps" are things I could not confirm.

Research method note: ~95 web fetches/searches, favouring primary docs and GitHub repos. Star counts are as displayed on GitHub on 15 Oct 2026 and are approximate. Many facts about the September–October 2026 "decision model" wave come from secondary press; those are flagged.

## Business rules and decision modelling: DMN, Drools/KIE, Camunda, OpenRules, IBM ODM / Oracle

### Takeaway
DMN gives the most mature vocabulary for *composing* decisions (decision requirements graphs, boxed expressions, decision tables with explicit hit policies, FEEL), but every construct is deterministic: there is no first-class notion of a probabilistic output, a confidence, or a learned threshold. Decision-table hit policies (Unique, Any, Priority, First, Rule Order, Output Order, Collect+aggregator) are a ready-made catalogue of "how to combine several matching judgments into one answer" that a compounding engine can generalize to probabilistic inputs.

### Cited Findings
- OMG lists DMN 1.6 with an adoption date of September 2026 and a DMN 1.7 beta published September 2026 "for informational purposes"; the notation shows how decisions depend on each other, on business knowledge and on input data, and the expression language "includes many convenient and expressive features, including decision tables"; models are exchanged as XML and are stated to be compatible with Java, PMML and ONNX — [OMG DMN spec page](https://www.omg.org/spec/DMN/)
- DMN decision requirements elements are Decision, Business Knowledge Model, Knowledge Source, Input Data and Decision Service, connected by information, knowledge and authority requirements; boxed expression kinds are decision table, literal expression, context, relation, function, invocation and list; FEEL ("Friendly Enough Expression Language") supports numbers, strings, three-valued booleans, dates/times/durations, ranges, lists, contexts and functions — [Drools DMN documentation](https://docs.drools.org/latest/drools-docs/drools/DMN/index.html)
- Drools' DMN engine supports DMN 1.1–1.4 at conformance level 3, and the editor supports DMN 1.2 at level 3; hit policies: Unique (one match, overlaps error), Any (several matches must agree), Priority (output chosen by position in the ordered output-values list), First (first matching rule in rule order), Collect with Sum/Max/Min/Count or plain list — [Drools DMN documentation](https://docs.drools.org/latest/drools-docs/drools/DMN/index.html)
- Camunda 8 DMN hit policies and result shapes: UNIQUE (default; two matches "fails and an incident is raised"), ANY (matches must produce the same output), FIRST, RULE ORDER (list in table order), COLLECT (unordered list), COLLECT+SUM/MIN/MAX/COUNT (single value; single output column required) — [Camunda 8 DMN hit policy docs](https://docs.camunda.io/docs/components/modeler/dmn/decision-table-hit-policy/)
- Camunda does not support the Priority or Output Order hit policies and suggests emulating them with Collect plus custom sorting — [Camunda best practice: choosing the DMN hit policy](https://docs.camunda.io/docs/components/best-practices/modeling/choosing-the-dmn-hit-policy/)
- Priority: "the matching rule with the highest output priority is returned", priorities given by the ordered list of output values; Output Order: "all hits are returned in decreasing output priority order" — [Trisotech, DMN hit policy explained](https://www.trisotech.com/dmn-hit-policy-explained/); OMG issue DMN12-149 recorded that the DMN 1.1 Output Order semantics were incorrect and fixed them in DMN 1.2 — [OMG issue DMN12-149](https://issues.omg.org/issues/DMN12-149)
- Formal semantics for DMN decision tables (including the ordering that defines Priority) were worked out academically — [Calvanese et al., Semantics and Analysis of DMN Decision Tables](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.07466); a practitioner notes "I have never seen hit policy O or R used in the wild" — [Bruce Silver via Trisotech](https://www.trisotech.com/?p=30077)
- Since October 2024 all Camunda 8 Self-Managed components (Zeebe, Operate, Tasklist, Optimize, Identity) ship under Camunda License 1.0: source available, free for development/testing, production requires a paid license; the Java client and BPMN model API remain Apache 2.0 — [Wikipedia: Camunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camunda); [Camunda forum on the community edition](https://forum.camunda.io/t/camunda-8-community-version-what-does-it-entail/37441)
- IBM Operational Decision Manager consists of Decision Center (rule management, including DMN-derived decision modelling since 8.10.5 and decision tables editable in Excel via Rule Solutions for Office) and Decision Server (execution); licensing is by edition (Decision Server Rule Edition, Express, Event Edition) — [IBM ODM Decision Center guide (PDF)](https://public.dhe.ibm.com/software/websphere/dmanager/PDF/86/odm_dcenter_bu_econsole_gs_ibmbook.pdf); [IBM ODM install guide (PDF)](https://public.dhe.ibm.com/software/websphere/dmanager/PDF/86/odm_distrib_install_ibmbook.pdf)
- The Gartner 2026 Decision Intelligence Platforms Magic Quadrant lists rules/decision-management vendors ACTICO, Decisions, FICO, FlexRule, IBM, InRule, Oracle, Pegasystems, Rulex, Sapiens and SAS among its evaluated vendors — [Martech Square summary of the MQ](https://martechsquare.substack.com/p/gartner-has-a-new-magic-quadrant)

### Inferences
- Borrow: the DRD as the shape of a *decision graph* (inputs → sub-decisions → decision; knowledge sources as provenance), the hit-policy catalogue as the vocabulary for combining several matched judgments, and FEEL's three-valued booleans (true/false/null) as precedent for "unknown/abstain" being a legitimate output.
- Borrow: Camunda's rule that a UNIQUE violation "raises an incident" is exactly the behaviour a compounding engine needs when two calibrated judgments conflict above threshold: surface a conflict rather than pick silently.
- Avoid: DMN's assumption that every cell is a crisp predicate. The spec has no slot for a probability, a confidence, a threshold that can be re-fitted, or an outcome feed-back path; adding those requires an extension layer, not a DMN profile.
- Avoid depending on Camunda 8's engine for an open-source core (Camunda License 1.0 forbids production use without a paid license); Drools remains the open implementation to borrow from.

### Gaps
- Drools' license is not stated on the fetched documentation page (it is widely reported as Apache 2.0 but I did not confirm it from a primary page).
- OpenRules: openrules.com redirects to OpenRules.ai and the fetched page had no content, so I could not confirm its product shape or license.
- Oracle's decision-management offerings were not researched beyond their inclusion in the Gartner vendor list.
- The DMN 1.6/1.7 spec PDFs were not read; hit-policy semantics above come from vendor docs and the DMN 1.2 issue tracker.

## Policy engines: Open Policy Agent (Rego), Cedar, Oso, Casbin

### Takeaway
Policy engines separate the *decision* from the *enforcement point*, return structured decisions with explanations (Cedar "determining policies", OPA arbitrary JSON plus decision logs), and encode conservative defaults (Cedar default-deny and forbid-overrides-permit; OPA `default` with `undefined` otherwise). OPA's decision-log schema is the closest existing thing to a standard "decision record" for machine decisions, but none of these engines represents uncertainty: outputs are booleans or documents, never probabilities.

### Cited Findings
- OPA decouples policy from services; decisions "are not limited to allow/deny" and can be arbitrary structured data; `input` is pushed synchronously with the query, `data` (base documents) is pushed or pulled via bundles, virtual documents are computed by rules — [OPA philosophy](https://www.openpolicyagent.org/docs/latest/philosophy/)
- Rego `default <name> := <term>` supplies a value "when all the rules sharing the same name are undefined"; a rule whose body never succeeds is `undefined`; complete rules yield one value (conflicts are errors), partial rules yield sets/objects; `every` over an empty domain is true — [OPA policy language](https://www.openpolicyagent.org/docs/latest/policy-language/)
- OPA decision logs: each event carries `decision_id`, `path`/`query`, `input`, `result`, `requested_by`, `bundles` with `revision`, `timestamp`, `metrics`, `trace_id`/`span_id`, `labels`, `rule_labels`, `erased`/`masked` JSON pointers and `nd_builtin_cache` (recording non-deterministic builtins such as `http.send` "for debugging and decision replay"); logs are gzip-batched to a service with retry/backoff, can be masked via `data.system.log.mask`, dropped via a `drop` rule, or printed to console; OPA is Apache 2.0 — [OPA decision logs](https://www.openpolicyagent.org/docs/latest/management-decision-logs/)
- Cedar policies have effect `permit`/`forbid`, a mandatory principal/action/resource scope and optional `when`/`unless` conditions; requests are PARC (principal, action, resource, context); the response is Allow/Deny plus the "determining policies"; templates can be linked; a JSON schema enables validation — [Cedar terminology](https://docs.cedarpolicy.com/overview/terminology.html)
- Cedar's algorithm: if any `forbid` is true → Deny; else if any `permit` is true → Allow; else Deny (default deny). A policy that errors is skipped and reported in diagnostics; `reason` lists the satisfied policies (empty on default deny), `errors` lists erroring policy IDs — [Cedar authorization](https://docs.cedarpolicy.com/auth/authorization.html)
- Cedar is Apache 2.0, implemented in Rust (~1.8k stars), "designed for analysis using Automated Reasoning", with a `cedar-policy-symcc` crate for verifying policy properties with counterexamples — [cedar-policy/cedar on GitHub](https://github.com/cedar-policy/cedar)
- Oso's open-source library (Polar policy language, Rust core, bindings for Node, Python, Go, Rust, Ruby, Java; Apache 2.0; ~3.5k stars) is deprecated but "not end-of-lifing"; Oso Cloud is the managed successor — [osohq/oso on GitHub](https://github.com/osohq/oso); the deprecation was announced 18 December 2023 — [Oso changelog](https://www.osohq.com/docs/oss/project/changelogs.html)
- Casbin (Apache 2.0, Go core with Java/Node/PHP/Python/.NET/C++/Rust ports, ~20.5k stars) uses the PERM metamodel (Policy, Effect, Request, Matchers); effect expressions such as `e = some(where (p.eft == allow))`; supports deny-override ("deny overrides the allow") and priority rules; adapters persist policy, watchers sync nodes — [casbin/casbin on GitHub](https://github.com/casbin/casbin)

### Inferences
- Borrow: OPA's decision-log event as the baseline schema for a decision record (decision id, input, result, policy version/revision, timestamp, metrics, masking markers, cached non-deterministic inputs for replay). Replace "bundle revision" with "model version + threshold set version" and add probabilities, confidence, threshold used, and a later outcome field.
- Borrow: Cedar's explicit three-way algorithm (forbid > permit > default deny) and its "skip-on-error, report in diagnostics" rule as the precedent for how a compounding engine should treat a failed or timed-out judgment: never let an error silently become an approval, always carry the error in the record.
- Borrow: Casbin's PERM idea that the *combination rule* (`e = ...`) is itself configuration. In a probabilistic engine the equivalent is the aggregation policy over several calibrated judgments (e.g., noisy-OR, min, weighted vote) being declared, versioned and tuned rather than hard-coded.
- Avoid: boolean-only outputs. None of the four has a slot for P(allow) or a confidence; a compounding engine must add that and keep the policy engine's determinism for the *final* enforcement step.
- Avoid: Oso's library as a dependency (deprecated); Cedar or OPA are the live open-source choices.

### Gaps
- OPA's implementation language and the location of its explain/trace documentation were not confirmed from fetched pages.
- Cedar's partial evaluation feature was not confirmed on the fetched pages.
- Casbin's PERM "Role definition" (`g`) section was not covered by the fetched page.

## Experimentation and feature-flag platforms as decision-rollout infrastructure

### Takeaway
Feature-flag platforms already solve *rollout of a decision rule*: percentage/segment targeting, kill switches, guarded rollouts that auto-roll-back on metric regression, and (via OpenFeature) a vendor-neutral evaluation API whose result object carries `value`, `variant`, `reason` and `errorCode`, with a hard rule that errors return the default value. What they do not have is a per-decision probability, a shadow/apprentice mode for *decisions*, or a standard for joining a decision to its outcome (OpenFeature's Tracking API is still experimental).

### Cited Findings
- OpenFeature is a CNCF incubating project; spec documentation is CC BY 4.0; spec sections are Flag Evaluation API, Provider, Evaluation Context, Hooks, Events, Tracking; unlabelled sections are Experimental — [OpenFeature specification](https://openfeature.dev/specification/)
- Evaluation details fields: `value`, `flagKey`, `variant`, `reason`, `errorCode`, `errorMessage`, `flagMetadata`; "Flag evaluation calls must always return the default value in the event of abnormal execution"; wrong-type provider values count as abnormal; hooks can be attached at API, client and invocation level — [OpenFeature flag evaluation](https://openfeature.dev/specification/sections/flag-evaluation)
- Standard reasons: STATIC, DEFAULT, TARGETING_MATCH, SPLIT (pseudorandom assignment), CACHED, DISABLED, UNKNOWN, STALE, ERROR; error codes: PROVIDER_NOT_READY, FLAG_NOT_FOUND, PARSE_ERROR, TYPE_MISMATCH, TARGETING_KEY_MISSING, INVALID_CONTEXT, PROVIDER_FATAL, GENERAL; provider statuses NOT_READY, READY, ERROR, STALE, FATAL, RECONCILING — [OpenFeature types](https://openfeature.dev/specification/types)
- Hooks have `before` (may mutate evaluation context), `after` (gets evaluation details), `error`, `finally`; before-stage order is API→Client→Invocation→Provider and reversed for the others; if a before hook fails the default value is returned; status "hardening" — [OpenFeature hooks](https://openfeature.dev/specification/sections/hooks)
- Tracking API (experimental): `track(tracking event name, evaluation context, tracking event details)` where details carry an optional numeric `value` and custom fields; purpose is to associate flag evaluations with later actions or business goals; no-op if a provider lacks tracking — [OpenFeature tracking](https://openfeature.dev/specification/sections/tracking)
- LaunchDarkly guarded rollouts (Enterprise plan + "Guardian add-on"): traffic to the new variation ramps to 100% while metrics are monitored; regression detection uses sequential testing on the absolute difference, flagged when the confidence interval lies entirely on the worse side of zero; the rollout can pause-and-notify or automatically roll back; if too few contexts are evaluated at a step LaunchDarkly rolls back automatically — [LaunchDarkly guarded rollouts](https://launchdarkly.com/docs/home/releases/guarded-rollouts); regression rules by metric direction and insufficient-traffic rollback — [LaunchDarkly managing guarded rollouts](https://launchdarkly.com/docs/home/releases/managing-guarded-rollouts)
- Statsig markets guarded releases "that automatically rollback based on metric thresholds" and staged rollouts with progression rules — [Statsig comparison page (vendor marketing)](https://statsig.com/comparison/alternatives-unleash-feature-flags); Statsig's Console API lets a CI pipeline "programmatically approve or halt rollout phases" for AI config rollouts — [Statsig blog](https://statsig.com/blog/automating-safe-ai-config-rollouts)
- OpenAI acquired Statsig in September 2025 (reported ~$1.1B) — [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/openai-acquires-statsig-for-a-reported-1-1-billion/); Datadog acquired Eppo in May 2025 (reported ~$220M) — [Dealroom](https://ai-startups.dealroom.co/news/feed/datadog-acquires-eppo-for-220m); [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2025/05/06/statsig-series-c-100-million-1-1-billion-eppo-datadog)
- Eppo's flags cover "A/B tests, feature gates, controlled rollouts, kill switches" — [Statsig comparison of Eppo and Unleash](https://statsig.com/perspectives/eppo-and-unleash-compared)
- GrowthBook: "the bulk of the code is under the permissive MIT license" with several directories under a GrowthBook Enterprise License; ~8.5k stars; feature flags with targeting and gradual rollouts, SQL-backed metrics, stats engine with CUPED, sequential, Bayesian, post-stratification, bandits and SRM checks; 24 SDKs — [growthbook/growthbook on GitHub](https://github.com/growthbook/growthbook)
- Unleash: GitHub shows AGPL-3.0 (README badge "AGPL-3.0-or-later"), ~13.9k stars; feature toggles with activation strategies, gradual rollouts/canary releases, kill-switch flag type, 2 environments in OSS — [Unleash/unleash on GitHub](https://github.com/Unleash/unleash); the LICENSE file is the GNU Affero General Public License v3 — [Unleash LICENSE](https://github.com/Unleash/unleash/blob/main/LICENSE); Unleash closed a $35M Series B in March 2026 — [tech-insider.org](https://tech-insider.org/?p=18557)

### Inferences
- Borrow: OpenFeature's evaluation-details object and its "never throw, always return the default" rule as the contract for a decision API: a decision engine's `decide()` should return value + reason + error code and degrade to a declared default (or to "ask a human") rather than raise.
- Borrow: the `reason` enumeration as the start of a decision-provenance vocabulary; extend it with reasons such as MODEL_ABOVE_THRESHOLD, MODEL_BELOW_THRESHOLD_ESCALATED, SHADOW, CACHED_DECISION, POLICY_OVERRIDE.
- Borrow: guarded-rollout mechanics (ramp, monitor a guardrail metric with sequential testing, auto-rollback, minimum sample per step) as the rollout mechanism for *a new threshold set or a new decision model*, treating the threshold set as the "variation".
- Avoid: building on Unleash without checking AGPL implications for a hosted product; several third-party pages still describe Unleash as Apache 2.0, which the current LICENSE file contradicts.
- Gap to fill: none of these platforms has a shadow mode for *decisions* (compute the new decision, log it, but act on the old one); the Tracking API is the only standard hook for joining a decision to an outcome and it is still experimental.

### Gaps
- I found no primary documentation of a shadow/dark-launch mode for decisions in LaunchDarkly, Statsig, Eppo, GrowthBook or Unleash; LaunchDarkly's and Statsig's docs describe guarded releases, not shadow evaluation.
- Eppo's current license/open-source status and Statsig's own guarded-release documentation were not found.
- LaunchDarkly SDK licenses were not checked.

## Bandits and online learning: Vowpal Wabbit, Personalizer, Decision Service, learning-loop, off-policy evaluation

### Takeaway
Contextual-bandit systems are the only mature prior art for *learning thresholds and policies from outcomes online*. The Decision Service formalized the loop as explore → log → learn → deploy with logged propensities enabling unbiased off-policy evaluation; Personalizer added "apprentice mode" (shadow learning against the incumbent policy) and a Rank/Reward API with delayed rewards. The commercial service is retiring in August 2026, leaving Vowpal Wabbit, microsoft/learning-loop and Open Bandit Pipeline as the open-source stack.

### Cited Findings
- Decision Service (Agarwal et al., 2016/2017) is "the first general system for contextual learning", built around four abstractions in a loop: "explore (the decision space), log, learn, and deploy"; explore and log produce "correct, unbiased data" for the learner, enabling online learning, real-time safeguards and reproducibility; deployments reported 25–30% click-through improvement and an 18% revenue gain — [arXiv 1606.03966](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.03966)
- Azure AI Personalizer: no new resources since 20 September 2023, service retiring 25 August 2026, migration recommended to microsoft/learning-loop; vocabulary: Context, Actions (with features), Reward in [0,1]; Rank API returns the best action per event ID, Reward API accepts (possibly delayed) reward; Apprentice mode "returns the baseline action or default action that the application would have taken" so the model learns by imitation before Online mode; recommends ≤~50 actions per call and ≥~1,000 events/day; offline (counterfactual) evaluation without user impact — [Microsoft Learn: What is Personalizer](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/personalizer/what-is-personalizer)
- Personalizer logs are DSJSON and can be re-evaluated offline with Vowpal Wabbit `--cb_adf` via SynapseML — [SynapseML contextual bandits](https://microsoft.github.io/SynapseML/docs/Explore%20Algorithms/Vowpal%20Wabbit/Contextual%20Bandits/)
- microsoft/learning-loop: "Joiner and Trainer for online reinforcement learning loop", MIT license, 13 stars, deploy-to-Azure guides — [microsoft/learning-loop on GitHub](https://github.com/microsoft/learning-loop)
- Vowpal Wabbit: BSD 3-Clause (PyPI, 9.11.9 released 27 Sep 2026) — [vowpalwabbit on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/vowpalwabbit/); ~8.7k stars, C++ — [VowpalWabbit/vowpal_wabbit on GitHub](https://github.com/VowpalWabbit/vowpal_wabbit); exploration algorithms explore-first, epsilon-greedy (default ε=0.05), bagging, online cover, softmax, RND, RegCB/SquareCB; `--cb_type` estimators IPS, DM, DR, MTR (default), SM; logged format `action:cost:probability` with a shared context line — [VW wiki: Contextual Bandit algorithms](https://github.com/VowpalWabbit/vowpal_wabbit/wiki/Contextual-Bandit-algorithms)
- Open Bandit Pipeline (ZOZO): Apache 2.0, Python, ~714 stars; OPE estimators Replay, DM, IPW, SNIPW, DR, Switch, MRDR, DRos, SGIPW/SGDR, DML, slate estimators (IIPS, RIPS, Cascade-DR), continuous-action kernels; Open Bandit Dataset logged under Uniform Random and Bernoulli Thompson Sampling for estimator comparison — [st-tech/zr-obp on GitHub](https://github.com/st-tech/zr-obp)

### Inferences
- Borrow: the explore/log/learn/deploy loop and the discipline of logging the *propensity* of the action taken. For a decision engine the analogue is to log, for every decision, the model probability, the threshold in force and whether the action was taken, randomized, shadowed or escalated, so off-policy estimators can evaluate a new threshold set before rollout.
- Borrow: Apprentice mode as the template for shadow mode: the engine returns the incumbent decision while the new policy's choice is recorded and scored.
- Borrow: Rank/Reward split with delayed rewards and event IDs as the decision/outcome join; the [0,1] scalar reward is too thin for multi-objective decisions and should be generalized to typed outcomes.
- Borrow: OBP's estimator suite for evaluating threshold changes offline (IPW/DR over logged decisions where exploration or randomized overrides supplied propensities).
- Avoid: pure bandit framing where the policy is a black box over actions; a compounding engine needs calibrated per-question probabilities as inputs, not just a learned argmax.

### Gaps
- learning-loop's internals (VW usage, supported reductions) are not described on its GitHub page.
- VW's repository page does not state the license; the BSD-3 statement comes from PyPI.
- I did not research Thompson sampling / LinUCB literature separately; VW's RND exploration is described as "related to LinUCB".

## "Decision intelligence" as a market category (Gartner): Quantexa, Peak, Aera, Diwo, Pyramid

### Takeaway
Gartner created a dedicated Magic Quadrant for Decision Intelligence Platforms on 26 January 2026, describing them as combining decision modelling, analytics and AI to augment and automate decisions across design, execution, monitoring and governance. The category treats a *decision* (not a model or a dashboard) as the managed asset, which is the right frame for a compounding engine, but the vendors are enterprise, closed-source, and oriented to large business decisions (pricing, supply chain, fraud) rather than high-frequency machine micro-decisions.

## LLM routers and cost/quality cascades: RouteLLM, FrugalGPT, Martian, Not Diamond, OpenRouter, Unify

### Takeaway
Routers and cascades are the existing prior art for *learned thresholds that trade cost against quality per query*: RouteLLM thresholds a learned P(strong wins | query) against α and calibrates α to a target strong-call fraction; FrugalGPT chains cheap-to-expensive models with a learned reliability scorer and per-stage acceptance thresholds. Both learn thresholds offline on a labelled set, neither recalibrates online from outcomes, and neither records decisions for audit.

### Cited Findings
- RouteLLM (Ong et al., arXiv 2406.18665, v4 Feb 2025): a router estimates P(win_s | q) and routes to the strong model when P ≥ α; metrics are cost (fraction of strong calls), PGR (performance gap recovered), APGR and CPT (call-performance threshold: minimum strong-call share for a target PGR); four routers: similarity-weighted ranking (Bradley-Terry weighted by embedding similarity), matrix factorization, BERT classifier, causal-LLM classifier (Llama 3 8B); trained on ~65k pruned Chatbot Arena comparisons augmented with ~1.5k MMLU golden labels and ~120k GPT-4-judged pairs (~$700); routers trained only on Arena data were near random on MMLU/GSM8K until augmented; routers generalize to new model pairs without retraining — [RouteLLM paper (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18665v4); abstract reports >2x cost reduction without quality loss — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18665)
- RouteLLM repo: Apache 2.0, ~5.6k stars; routers `mf` (recommended), `sw_ranking`, `bert`, `causal_llm`, `random`; `calibrate_threshold --strong-model-pct 0.5` returns a threshold for a target strong-call share (examples show 0.11593 and 0.1881 for the same 50% target); OpenAI-compatible server; model names `router-[ROUTER]-[THRESHOLD]`; evals on MT Bench, MMLU, GSM8K — [lm-sys/RouteLLM on GitHub](https://github.com/lm-sys/RouteLLM)
- FrugalGPT (Chen, Zaharia, Zou, May 2023): three strategies — prompt adaptation, LLM approximation (completion cache, fine-tuning), LLM cascade; the cascade is an ordered list of m LLMs (length 3 studied) with a scoring function g(q, a) ∈ [0,1] (a DistilBERT regression model trained to predict answer correctness) and per-stage thresholds τ_i; the first answer whose score clears its threshold is returned, the last model always answers; example learned cascade GPT-J (accept > 0.96) → J1-L (accept > 0.37) → GPT-4; list and thresholds are optimized jointly to maximize expected quality under a cost budget b, using pruning of low-disagreement lists and interpolation; cost reductions of 98.3% (HEADLINES vs GPT-4), 73.3% (OVERRULING), 59.2% (COQA) at matched accuracy, and up to 4–5% accuracy gain at equal cost — [FrugalGPT via ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.05176); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176)
- Commercial routers (secondary sources): Martian is a hosted router claiming 20–97% cost reduction — [LLM Reference routers list](https://www.llmreference.com/routers); Not Diamond acts as a recommender rather than a proxy, lets users train custom routers on their own evals, and is said to power OpenRouter's auto mode — [fast.io](https://www.fast.io/resources/best-llm-routing-platforms-agents.md), [SourceForge listing](https://sourceforge.net/software/llm-routers/saas/); OpenRouter is a gateway (300+ models, failover, 5.5% fee) classified as availability/price routing rather than learned routing — [Holori](https://holori.com/best-llm-routers/); Unify predicts quality with a neural scoring function and refreshes benchmarks every 10 minutes — [SourceForge listing](https://sourceforge.net/software/llm-routers/saas/)

### Inferences
- Borrow: RouteLLM's separation of *estimate* (P(win)) from *policy* (α) and its calibrate-to-a-budget utility; the compounding engine's per-question thresholds are the same object and should be fit the same way (target a cost or escalation rate, not an arbitrary 0.5).
- Borrow: FrugalGPT's cascade with a cheap scorer and per-stage thresholds; a decision model such as Jev/Clef is a natural cheap first stage, and the "last stage always answers" rule should become "last stage is a human or a frontier model".
- Avoid: training routers on preference data alone (RouteLLM's near-random MMLU result) — thresholds and routers must be fit on labelled outcomes for the task at hand.
- Gap: neither router nor cascade logs decisions, tracks calibration drift, or re-fits thresholds from production outcomes; both are offline-trained components, not a decision ledger.

### Gaps
- Primary pricing/API docs for Martian, Not Diamond, Unify and OpenRouter auto-routing were not fetched; figures above are from aggregators.
- Whether Not Diamond actually powers OpenRouter's auto mode is a secondary claim.

## LLM-as-judge and evaluation frameworks: DeepEval, Ragas, Promptfoo, Braintrust, Langfuse, Arize Phoenix, LangSmith

### Takeaway
Eval frameworks give a stable vocabulary — test case, metric/scorer, score in [0,1] with rationale metadata, per-metric threshold for pass/fail, dataset, experiment, trace — and they already treat an LLM judge's score as a thresholded decision. They are offline/CI-oriented: no calibration of the judge, no cost-aware escalation, no join to production outcomes beyond annotations.

### Cited Findings
- DeepEval: Apache 2.0, Python, ~18.9k stars; `LLMTestCase` (input, actual_output, expected_output, retrieval_context); metrics G-Eval, Answer Relevancy, Faithfulness, Hallucination, DAG and custom; every metric takes a `threshold` (scores 0–1 determine pass/fail); pytest integration; synthetic datasets; `@observe()` tracing for component-level evals — [confident-ai/deepeval on GitHub](https://github.com/confident-ai/deepeval)
- Ragas: Apache 2.0, Python, ~16.0k stars; LLM-based and traditional metrics, `DiscreteMetric` for custom evaluators with allowed values, test-set generation, "feedback loops" from production data — [explodinggradients/ragas on GitHub](https://github.com/explodinggradients/ragas)
- Braintrust autoevals: MIT, Python and TypeScript, ~1.1k stars; LLM-as-judge scorers (Battle, ClosedQA, Factuality, Moderation, Security, Summarization, SQL, Translation), heuristic scorers (Levenshtein, exact match, numeric diff, JSON diff), RAG metrics; each returns a `Score` with `name`, `score` (0–1 or null when skipped) and `metadata` such as `rationale` — [braintrustdata/autoevals on GitHub](https://github.com/braintrustdata/autoevals); the Braintrust platform itself is described as closed, without an open-source core — [FutureAGI comparison (competitor marketing)](https://futureagi.com/blog/best-promptfoo-alternatives-2026)
- Arize Phoenix: Elastic License 2.0, ~11.9k stars; OpenTelemetry/OpenInference tracing, LLM evals, versioned datasets, experiments, prompt playground — [Arize-ai/phoenix on GitHub](https://github.com/Arize-ai/phoenix)
- Langfuse core is MIT with some enterprise features behind a license key; ClickHouse acquired Langfuse in January 2026 and said it would keep the MIT license — [PostHog comparison](https://posthog.com/compare/best-ai-evaluation-tools-for-production); [Dupple review](https://dupple.com/learn/best-ai-evaluation-tools)
- Promptfoo's repo is reported as MIT — [FutureAGI (competitor marketing)](https://futureagi.com/blog/best-promptfoo-alternatives-2026)

### Inferences
- Borrow: the `Score {name, score, metadata.rationale}` shape and DeepEval's per-metric threshold as the minimal "judgment" record; a compounding engine should treat an LLM judge as just another probabilistic judgment source with its own calibration curve.
- Borrow: Ragas' `DiscreteMetric` with allowed values — it is structurally the same as a Jev `Choice` question and could be backed by a decision model.
- Avoid: ELv2 (Phoenix) if the engine needs to be redistributed as a service; prefer Apache 2.0/MIT components (DeepEval, Ragas, autoevals, Langfuse core).
- Gap: none of these frameworks measures judge calibration (ECE/Brier) or learns judge thresholds from downstream outcomes; thresholds are hand-set constants.

### Gaps
- Promptfoo and Langfuse licenses were not confirmed from their repositories.
- LangSmith (commercial) was not researched; its SDK license is unconfirmed.
- Ragas' specific RAG metrics (faithfulness, context precision/recall) were not visible on the fetched page.

## Guardrail frameworks: NeMo Guardrails, Guardrails AI, Llama Guard, OpenAI Moderation and OpenAI Guardrails, Anthropic constitutional classifiers

### Takeaway
Guardrails are the most developed prior art for *gating* a step: they standardize where a check runs (input, dialog, retrieval, execution, output rails), what happens on failure (Guardrails AI's `on_fail` menu: reask, fix, filter, refrain, noop, exception, fix_reask; OpenAI's "tripwire" that blocks or only flags), and they return per-category scores (OpenAI Moderation `category_scores`). What they lack is calibration of those scores, learned thresholds, and a record that joins the gate decision to what happened next.

### Cited Findings
- NVIDIA NeMo Guardrails: Apache 2.0, Python, ~7.3k stars; Colang 1.0 (default) and 2.0 for dialogue flows; five rail types — input, dialog, retrieval, execution (inputs/outputs of custom actions/tools) and output; rails are flows in `.co` files enabled in `config.yml`; server exposes `/v1/chat/completions`; LangChain integration — [NVIDIA/NeMo-Guardrails on GitHub](https://github.com/NVIDIA/NeMo-Guardrails)
- Guardrails AI: Apache 2.0, Python (JS supported), ~7.5k stars; `Guard().use(...)` with validators from the Guardrails Hub; input and output checks; Pydantic-structured outputs — [guardrails-ai/guardrails on GitHub](https://github.com/guardrails-ai/guardrails); `on_fail` actions: REASK (prompt the LLM with the failure criteria), FIX (programmatic repair, e.g., strip hallucinated sentences), FILTER (drop the failing structured field), REFRAIN (return None), EXCEPTION, FIX_REASK (fix, re-validate, then reask), NOOP (log only) — [Guardrails AI on_fail policies](https://guardrailsai.com/docs/hub/concepts/on_fail_policies); [use on_fail actions](https://guardrailsai.com/docs/how_to_guides/use_on_fail_actions)
- Llama Guard 4 12B: Llama 4 Community License; classifies prompts and responses against the MLCommons hazard taxonomy (S1–S14); text and multi-image input; output is generated text "safe" or "unsafe" plus violated category codes — [meta-llama/Llama-Guard-4-12B on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Llama-Guard-4-12B)
- OpenAI Moderation: `omni-moderation-latest`, text and image input, free; output `flagged` boolean, `categories` booleans, `category_scores` (0–1 confidence), `category_applied_input_types`; guidance: start from `flagged`, use scores "for logging, routing, or review queues", treat scores as signals for your own policy, and expect recalibration when the model updates — [OpenAI moderation guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/moderation)
- OpenAI Guardrails (openai/openai-guardrails-python): MIT, "Python (Preview)", drop-in wrapper for the OpenAI client and Agents SDK; JSON config from the guardrails.openai.com wizard; blocked requests raise `GuardrailTripwireTriggered` (Agents SDK: `InputGuardrailTripwireTriggered`/`OutputGuardrailTripwireTriggered`); 12 built-in checks (Keyword Filter, Competitors, Moderation, URL Filter, Secret Keys, Contains PII, Hallucination Detection, Jailbreak, Prompt Injection Detection, NSFW Text, Off Topic Prompts, Custom Prompt Check); guardrail calls are billed OpenAI API calls — [openai/openai-guardrails-python on GitHub](https://github.com/openai/openai-guardrails-python); tripwires can "block or mask unsafe content, or only log and flag it" — [repo commit](https://github.com/openai/openai-guardrails-python/commit/1e46404b9bffab24ed7869706cc18dfe4bfc39d5); Guardrails shipped with AgentKit, announced 6 October 2025 — [Unite.ai cybersecurity coverage](https://www.unite.ai/es/cybersecurity/)
- Anthropic constitutional classifiers (arXiv 2501.18837, 31 Jan 2025, Sharma et al.): safeguards trained on synthetic data generated from a natural-language constitution; over 3,000 hours of red teaming no universal jailbreak was found against the guarded model; 0.38% absolute increase in production refusals; 23.7% inference overhead — [arXiv 2501.18837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18837)
- The open-source LLM Guard project was archived in July 2026 — [GetMaxim guardrails roundup](https://getmaxim.ai/articles/best-ai-guardrails-platforms-in-2026/)

### Inferences
- Borrow: NeMo's rail taxonomy (where a gate sits in the pipeline) and Guardrails AI's `on_fail` menu (what a gate does on failure) as the engine's gate vocabulary; add `escalate` (route to a stronger judge or human) and `shadow` (record but do not act) as first-class outcomes, which no framework has.
- Borrow: OpenAI Moderation's `category_scores` + explicit advice to recalibrate thresholds when the model changes — an argument that threshold fitting must be a repeatable, versioned operation (as ReAnchor does) rather than hand-tuning.
- Avoid: guardrail outputs that are generated text ("unsafe S9") rather than probabilities; Llama Guard-style outputs cannot be thresholded or calibrated without extra work.
- Note the cost asymmetry: constitutional classifiers add ~24% compute; a cheap calibrated decision model as a first-stage gate is the FrugalGPT pattern applied to safety.

### Gaps
- I found no evidence of a *2026* OpenAI Guardrails release; the only OpenAI Guardrails artefacts found are the October 2025 AgentKit launch and the MIT Python repo (no releases listed on the fetched page). The "2026 OpenAI Guardrails" named in the brief could not be confirmed.
- Guardrails AI's `ValidationOutcome` structure and NeMo's Colang 2.0 details were not visible on the fetched pages.

## Agent middleware and hooks: LangChain middleware (langchain-typesafe), Vercel AI SDK decisions, Claude Code hooks, Cloudflare Agents

### Takeaway
Agent frameworks have converged on a middleware/hook point around every tool call and model call, and in 2026 they began wiring decision models into it: LangChain's `langchain-typesafe` ships `ModelRouterMiddleware` (Jev `Choice` picks the model) and `AutoModeMiddleware` (Jev `Noul` risk probability blocks tool calls above a threshold); Vercel's AI SDK has `experimental_decide` with choice/score/boolean/box questions across TypeSafe, OpenAI's native Decisions API and prompted fallbacks; Claude Code's `PreToolUse` hook returns `allow`/`deny`/`ask`/`defer` with a reason and can be a prompt- or agent-evaluated hook; Cloudflare Agents make approval a durable wait. None of them records the probability, threshold and later outcome in a ledger, and thresholds are static configuration.

### Cited Findings
- `langchain-typesafe` 0.0.1a3 (released 20 Sep 2026, MIT, Python ≥3.10, "may not be stable for production use"): `TypeSafeClassifier` Runnable (state + questions; `Choice`, `Noul`, `Score`); `ModelRouterMiddleware` (experimental) routes an agent to a model selected by a TypeSafe `Choice` over configured `ModelChoice` entries, classifying the latest human message once per run and storing the full `ChoiceAnswer` (probabilities, confidence) in agent state; `AutoModeMiddleware` (experimental) classifies calls to listed tools with a `NoulCriteria` (risky/safe) and returns an error `ToolMessage` when risk probability meets or exceeds a threshold; no default threshold or fallback documented — [langchain-typesafe on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/langchain-typesafe/); the 0.0.1a2 release notes list both as experimental additions — [release mirror](https://prismix.dev/news/aa2e5507de45)
- LangChain's middleware integrations page lists provider middleware (OpenAI moderation; Microsoft Foundry moderation/prompt shield/groundedness; NVIDIA model routing) and dozens of community guardrail/authorization middlewares, e.g., CTRLRunMiddleware ("policy-gated tool calls, at-most-once effects, human approval, receipts"), attenu-guard (hash-chained audit log), AxioRank (scores tool calls to allow/deny/redact), and MeragptDecider ("typed decisions (yes/no, choice, score) with calibrated probabilities") — [LangChain middleware integrations](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/integrations/middleware)
- Vercel AI SDK `experimental_decide({ model, state, questions })`: state is a string, JSON or ordered parts (text/file/json); question types `choice` (criteria map → `choice` + optional `probabilities`), `score` (≥2 ordered levels → fractional `score` + optional `probabilities`), `boolean` (→ required `probability`), `box` (→ normalized image bounding box); providers: `typeSafeAi.decisionModel('jev-latest')` (native), `openai.decisionModel('gpt-6-luna')` ("uses the native Decisions API"), Anthropic and Google via structured output (no distributions; boolean probability estimated by prompting); "experimental and may change in patch releases" — [AI SDK evaluation/decision docs](https://ai-sdk.dev/docs/ai-sdk-core/evaluation); Sentry documents tracing of `experimental_evaluate()` calls in AI SDK 7 — [Sentry Vercel AI integration](https://docs.sentry.io/platforms/javascript/guides/mastra/configuration/integrations/vercelai/); AI Gateway exposes the model string `typesafe-ai/jev` — [Vercel AI Gateway evaluation quickstart](https://vercel.com/docs/ai-gateway/getting-started/evaluation)
- Claude Code hooks: events include `PreToolUse` (can block), `PermissionRequest`, `PostToolUse`, `PostToolUseFailure`, `UserPromptSubmit`, `Stop`, `SubagentStop`; input JSON carries `session_id`, `cwd`, `permission_mode`, `hook_event_name`, `tool_name`, `tool_input`, `tool_use_id`; PreToolUse output `hookSpecificOutput.permissionDecision` ∈ {`allow`, `deny`, `ask`, `defer`} with `permissionDecisionReason` and optional `updatedInput`; exit code 2 blocks (stderr is the reason), 0 with no output means "no decision"; handler types `command`, `http`, `mcp_tool`, `prompt` (single-turn model evaluation, 30s default timeout) and `agent` (subagent with Read/Grep/Glob, 60s, experimental); a timed-out hook does not block unless `onFailure: "block"` — [Claude Code hooks reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks)
- Cloudflare Agents human-in-the-loop: `waitForApproval()` is a durable gate backed by Cloudflare Workflows that can wait months or years without keeping an Agent running; Code Mode approvals via `requiresApproval`, `approve()`, `reject()`; MCP elicitation via `configureElicitationHandlers()` — [Cloudflare Agents HITL patterns](https://developers.cloudflare.com/agents/concepts/agentic-patterns/human-in-the-loop/); Cloudflare Workflows reached GA as durable execution — [Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/workflows-ga-production-ready-durable-execution/)

### Inferences
- Borrow: Claude Code's four-valued decision (`allow`/`deny`/`ask`/`defer`) plus a mandatory reason — it is the most complete gate-outcome vocabulary found, and "ask" is exactly the escalate-to-human branch a calibrated threshold should trigger below a confidence band.
- Borrow: the `prompt`/`agent` hook idea (a model evaluates the gate) but back it with a decision model returning a probability rather than a free-text verdict, so the gate can be calibrated.
- Borrow: Vercel's provider abstraction (native decision models vs prompted fallbacks that "omit distributions") — a compounding engine needs the same adapter boundary and should mark non-native probabilities as uncalibrated.
- Avoid: `AutoModeMiddleware`-style fixed thresholds in configuration with no record of the probability observed; the probability, threshold version and result must be logged or nothing can be learned.
- Durable approval waits (Cloudflare) are the right substrate for the "ask" branch when humans are slow.

### Gaps
- The name discrepancy `experimental_evaluate` (brief, Sentry docs, third-party blogs) vs `experimental_decide` (current AI SDK docs) suggests a rename; I could not confirm when or whether both exist.
- LangChain's official reference lists neither `AutoModeMiddleware` nor `ModelRouterMiddleware`; they exist only in the separate `langchain-typesafe` package.
- The brief named "LangChain middleware incl. AutoModeMiddleware and ModelRouterMiddleware" as if core; they are experimental, alpha and vendor-specific.

## Decision-model products: TypeSafe Jev, Cloudflare Clef, Amazon Strands Decider, OpenAI Decisions API, the Jev Decision Index

### Takeaway
Since mid-September 2026 a new product class has formed around a shared API: a `state` plus typed `questions` (yes/no "noul"/predicate/boolean, `choice` over described options, `score` over ordered levels), returning probabilities and a confidence rather than text. Jev (proprietary, early access) defined the shape; Cloudflare Clef/Clef-flash (Apache 2.0 weights, "fully Jev-API compatible"), Amazon's Strands Decider family (Apache 2.0, 2B–26B) and OpenAI's Decisions API (`POST /v1/decisions`, gpt-6-luna, public beta 6 Oct 2026) followed within weeks, and open leaderboards (Jev Decision Index, JevBench) appeared. Every vendor explicitly delegates thresholding, composition and escalation to "your code".

### Cited Findings
- TypeSafe AI describes "System One Models" as a model class "built for decisions inside software", trained with "Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions (RLCD)", a new architecture and sampler; Jev returns typed decisions with probabilities and confidence; developers "set thresholds for when it acts autonomously and when it asks for review, and they can combine decisions in code"; claims "193.6x Faster, 444.6x Cheaper" and "Zero Hallucinations"; listed price $42 per billion input tokens; docs at docs.typesafe.ai — [typesafe.ai](https://typesafe.ai/)
- TypeSafe docs: one request = state ("text or JSON") + one or more questions evaluated "in parallel and in isolation"; Choice returns `choice`, `probabilities`, `confidence`; Score returns `score`, `probabilities`, `confidence`; Noul returns `noul` (0–1); guidance: ask one well-scoped thing per question, split multi-factor judgments into separate questions and "combine the answers with your own logic" — [TypeSafe docs introduction](https://docs.typesafe.ai/introduction)
- Jev via OpenRouter: state may be string, JSON or array of text (no images/audio); the question id is not sent to the model; Choice → `choice` + `probabilities` map + `confidence`; Score → probability-weighted average of level indices + `legend` + per-level `probabilities` + `confidence` (≤10 levels); Noul → single probability, no confidence field; calibration claim "when Jev outputs 0.8, it is right about that kind of answer roughly 80% of the time" in aggregate; confidence "reflects how concentrated the probability distribution is, not whether the answer is correct"; probabilities vary slightly between identical calls so "threshold on bands rather than exact values"; price $0.042/M input, $0 output (as of 21 Sep 2026); 32k context on OpenRouter (TypeSafe docs: 64k per request, 32k for state); model ids `typesafe/jev-1.13` and `~typesafe/jev-latest`; endpoint `POST /api/alpha/decisions`; recommended uses: routing/triage, classification, "gating agent actions, such as using a Noul check before a destructive tool call", verifying LLM drafts against policy, ranking; limitations: no text/rationale, no tools, text-only, bad at arithmetic/date math, proprietary with no weights or paper — [OpenRouter: What is Jev](https://openrouter.ai/blog/insights/what-is-jev/)
- Secondary coverage of Jev's launch: early access from 15 September 2026; 70–500 ms latency and $0.042/M input claimed; on TypeSafe's own four-workflow benchmark Jev scored 67.8% vs 74.1% for GPT-5.6 Sol and 73.1% for Claude Opus 5 — [Sanity glossary](https://www.sanity.io/glossary/jev-typesafe-ai-model); [BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/blog/posts/what-is-jev); the "cannot hallucinate" claim is treated as a launch claim pending independent tests — [Beam AI](https://beam.ai/agentic-insights/jev-typesafe-ai-agents)

- Clef on Workers AI: `@cf/cloudflare/clef` (27B multimodal) and `@cf/cloudflare/clef-flash`; 65,536-token window shared by media, state and questions (state truncated to fit); 1–64 questions per request keyed by id; question types `noul` (instructions), `choice` (instructions + criteria map), `score` (instructions + ordered criteria list); optional `images` (≤4, PNG/JPEG/WebP) as an extension to the "System One" API; answers: `noul` → probability, `choice` → option + probability per option, `score` → probability-weighted score; $0.24/M input tokens, output free; images billed as input tokens (≤1,024 per image) — [Cloudflare Workers AI Clef model page](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/models/clef/)

- OpenAI Decisions API (secondary): limited preview at DevDay (29 Sep 2026), public beta 6 October 2026, GA "in the coming weeks"; `POST /v1/decisions` with `model`, `input` (text or messages with inline base64 images) and `questions` (name, type, instructions, choices or score levels); answers: Predicate → probability 0–1; Choice → selected value, probability per option, separate confidence; Score → probability-weighted average of level indices plus confidence; only `gpt-6-luna`; "about 10x faster than the Responses API"; $0.10/M input tokens, output and cache not billed; dependent decisions need separate requests — [Unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/openai-releases-decisions-api-in-public-beta-powered-by-gpt-6-luna/); one outlet notes gpt-6-luna's endpoint table omits v1/decisions — [Mixed News](https://mixed-news.com/en/openai-decisions-api-beta-gpt-6-luna-endpoint-table/); OpenAI's DevDay recap — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/29/openai-devday-2026-live-updates.html)

- JevBench (Benchmark Heaven) is a different composite of Intelligence, Calibration, Speed and Cost; v1.5.4 shows Cygnet (73.70) and Winnow-12B Q8 (73.23) tied at the top; another board shows Quyet-1.0-Large at 71.4 on v1.6.0, so versions are not comparable — [BenchLM JevBench](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/jevbench); [LLM Reference JevBench](https://www.llmreference.com/benchmark/jevbench)

### Inferences
- The de-facto wire format is now stable enough to build on: `{state, questions: {id: {type: noul|choice|score, instructions, criteria}}} → {answers: {id: {probability | choice+probabilities+confidence | score+probabilities+confidence}}}`. Clef, Strands and OpenAI all converge on it; Vercel and DSPy already abstract over it. A compounding engine should adopt this as its judgment interface and treat Jev/Clef/Decider/OpenAI/prompted-LLM as swappable backends with per-backend calibration records.
- Every vendor stops at the single question. TypeSafe's own docs tell users to split judgments and recombine "in code"; OpenAI says dependent decisions need separate requests; Cloudflare evaluates questions "in isolation". Composition, thresholding, escalation and learning are explicitly out of scope for the models — that is the gap the engine fills.
- Calibration claims are vendor claims. Clef trains with a Brier loss; Jev claims aggregate calibration; neither publishes reliability diagrams per question type. The engine must measure calibration itself from recorded outcomes.
- Confidence is defined differently across the stack: Jev's `confidence` is distribution concentration (not correctness); DSPy's Noul `confidence` is distance from the threshold. The engine should carry raw probabilities and derive its own confidence notion rather than trusting a field called "confidence".
- Latency/cost (tens to hundreds of ms, cents per million tokens, free output) make it feasible to ask several redundant questions per step and to re-ask on near-0.5 results, which is the raw material for compounding.

### Gaps
- TypeSafe's Primitives/Confidence/Patterns doc pages, Jev's model card and any calibration evaluation were not fetched; the "Noul" term's origin is undocumented in fetched sources.
- Strands Decider's primary launch post and model card were not fetched; license and architecture come from secondary sources.
- OpenAI's own Decisions API documentation was not fetched; shape and pricing are from secondary press.
- Jev Decision Index and JevBench rankings were not read from the primary leaderboards; the numbers above are secondary and version-dependent.
- Whether Clef exposes a `confidence` field: the blog implies compatibility, but the Workers AI page describes no separate confidence field.

## Durable execution and ledgers for checkpointed multi-step decisions: Temporal, Inngest, Restate, Azure Durable Functions, Cloudflare Workflows, event sourcing

### Takeaway
Durable-execution engines make long chains of steps resumable by recording every step as an event and replaying deterministic code against the history, which doubles as a complete audit log. They solve the *reliability* half of the 0.8^n chain problem (a crashed or failed step is retried and resumed, never silently lost) but have no notion of a *probabilistic* step: nothing tracks the accumulated confidence of a chain, decides when to insert a verification step, or budgets re-checks.

### Cited Findings
- Temporal: "Once a Workflow starts, it runs to completion, whether that takes a second or a year"; Workflow code must be deterministic because it is replayed to rebuild state; Activities hold all external effects and are supervised with retries, timeouts and heartbeats; the SDK records Commands and the service appends Events to an Event History, "a complete, ordered record of everything that happened"; Workers poll Task Queues ("Workers do not receive work; they ask for it"); Signals/Queries/Updates; Worker Versioning pins running executions to their original code — [Temporal: Understanding Temporal](https://docs.temporal.io/evaluate/understanding-temporal); server is MIT, Go, ~23.7k stars — [temporalio/temporal on GitHub](https://github.com/temporalio/temporal)
- Inngest: server and CLI under the Server Side Public License with delayed open-source publication to Apache 2.0, SDKs Apache 2.0; ~5.9k stars; functions triggered by events/cron/webhooks, `step.run` retried per step, `step.waitForEvent` pauses until a matching event, flow control (concurrency, throttling, debounce, rate limit, priority, batching) — [inngest/inngest on GitHub](https://github.com/inngest/inngest)
- Restate's server is Business Source License 1.1 converting to Apache 2.0 after four years (secondary) — [youngju.dev durable execution survey](https://www.youngju.dev/blog/culture/2026-05-14-durable-execution-engines-2026-temporal-restate-inngest-trigger-dev-dbos-deep-dive-2026)
- Azure Durable Functions extension: MIT, C#, ~766 stars; orchestrator functions coordinate activity functions (stateless) and entity functions (stateful, actor-like) — [Azure/azure-functions-durable-extension on GitHub](https://github.com/Azure/azure-functions-durable-extension)
- Cloudflare Workflows persist state, retry steps and can "wait on external events: webhooks, approvals, queue messages" — [Cloudflare Workflows product page](https://cloudflare.com/products/workflows/); GA announcement — [Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/workflows-ga-production-ready-durable-execution/)
- Event Sourcing (Fowler): "Capture all changes to an application state as a sequence of events"; enables Complete Rebuild, Temporal Query and Event Replay; wrap external systems in Gateways that skip outbound calls during replay and record external query responses for reuse; serializing the event log "produces an audit log" — [Martin Fowler, Event Sourcing](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html)

### Inferences
- Borrow: the Event History pattern — every judgment, threshold comparison, escalation and outcome appended as an immutable event, with the engine's state (current thresholds, calibration tables) rebuildable by replay. OPA's `nd_builtin_cache` and Fowler's "record external query responses for replay" are the same idea; cache each decision model's raw response so a chain can be replayed deterministically after a model or threshold change.
- Borrow: Temporal's Worker Versioning semantics for threshold sets — in-flight decision chains finish under the policy they started with; new chains pick up the new one.
- Borrow: `waitForEvent`/`waitForApproval` as the implementation of the "ask a human" branch, so low-confidence decisions suspend rather than block.
- Gap: none of these engines knows a step has a probability. The compounding engine should sit *inside* a durable workflow as the thing that decides, per step, whether to proceed, re-ask, cross-check or escalate — and should record the chain's running confidence as workflow state.

### Gaps
- Restate's license and Inngest's primary language were not confirmed from primary pages.
- Temporal's retry-policy parameters and Azure Durable Functions' replay/determinism rules were not visible on the fetched pages.

## Decision records and journals: ADRs (Nygard), MADR, decision journals, operational decision logs

### Takeaway
The human-decision literature already prescribes what a decision record should contain — context, options considered, the decision, status with supersession rather than editing, consequences, and (in decision journals) the expected outcome with probabilities and a later outcome review. Machine-side, OPA's decision log is the only widely used schema. No standard combines both: a record with inputs, the probabilistic judgment, the threshold/policy version, the action taken, and the eventual outcome.

### Cited Findings
- Nygard's ADR: sections Title, Context (forces "in tension", stated neutrally), Decision ("We will…"), Status (proposed, accepted, deprecated, superseded), Consequences (positive, negative, neutral); numbers never reused; "If a decision is reversed, we will keep the old one around, but mark it as superseded"; one or two pages at `doc/arch/adr-NNN.md`; motivation: newcomers otherwise "blindly accept" or "blindly reverse" decisions — [Nygard, Documenting Architecture Decisions](https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions)
- MADR 4.0.0 (2024-09-17, MIT OR CC0-1.0): fields Context and Problem Statement, Decision Drivers, Considered Options, Decision Outcome, Consequences, Confirmation ("how compliance with the decision will be checked"), Pros and Cons of the Options, More Information (evidence, revisit conditions, links); YAML metadata status (proposed, rejected, accepted, deprecated, superseded), date, decision-makers, consulted, informed — [MADR](https://adr.github.io/madr/)
- Farnam Street decision journal fields: situation/context, problem statement/frame, governing variables, complications, alternatives seriously considered and why rejected, range of possible outcomes, expected outcome with reasoning and "assigned probabilities, including confidence level", time of day and physical/mental state; purpose: "recording what you knew and what you thought at the time" to combat hindsight bias — [Farnam Street, Decision Journal](https://fs.blog/decision-journal/)
- OPA decision log events (fields listed in the policy-engine section) are described as supporting "auditing and offline debugging of policy decisions" and decision replay — [OPA decision logs](https://www.openpolicyagent.org/docs/latest/management-decision-logs/)
- Personalizer's Rank/Reward model ties each decision to a unique event ID so a (possibly delayed) outcome can be joined later — [Microsoft Learn: What is Personalizer](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/personalizer/what-is-personalizer)

### Inferences
- Borrow: supersession-not-mutation (ADR), Confirmation (MADR: how compliance is checked) and the decision journal's "assigned probabilities + later outcome review" as the three non-obvious fields of a machine decision record. Status values map directly: proposed = shadow, accepted = live, deprecated/superseded = threshold set retired.
- A machine decision record should therefore contain: decision id, question(s) and criteria version, state digest, backend/model version, raw probabilities, derived confidence, threshold/policy version, decision taken, mode (live/shadow/escalated/overridden), timestamp, cost/latency, and a later outcome event joined by id.
- Avoid: free-text-only records (ADR style) for machine decisions — they are unqueryable for calibration.

### Gaps
- I found no published standard for "decision logs" in operations/SRE beyond ADRs and OPA; no source found on confidence fields in operational decision-log templates.

## Calibration and monitoring tooling: reliability diagrams, ECE, Brier, conformal prediction, drift

### Takeaway
Open-source tooling exists for every measurement a compounding engine needs — ECE and reliability metrics, post-hoc calibration (temperature/isotonic scaling), conformal prediction sets with coverage guarantees (MAPIE, crepes, TorchUncertainty), drift detection (Evidently) and, most relevant, label-free performance estimation from calibrated probabilities (NannyML CBPE). All are Python libraries aimed at classical ML models; none is wired to LLM/decision-model outputs or to a decision ledger.

### Cited Findings
- MAPIE (scikit-learn-contrib): BSD 3-Clause; conformal prediction intervals and sets for regression, classification and time series; depends only on scikit-learn and numpy — [MAPIE on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/MAPIE/0.9.0); repo — [scikit-learn-contrib/MAPIE](https://github.com/simai-ml/MAPIE)
- crepes: conformal classifiers, regressors and predictive systems; BSD-3-Clause (v0.9.0 per DepScope) — [crepes on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/crepes/); [DepScope listing](https://depscope.dev/pkg/pypi/crepes); the crepes-weighted extension is also BSD-3 — [crepes-weighted on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/crepes-weighted/0.1.0)
- TorchUncertainty: Apache 2.0, PyTorch, ~532 stars; Deep Ensembles, MC-Dropout, Packed-Ensembles, variational BNNs; calibration metrics ECE and SmoothECE; temperature, vector and matrix scaling; conformal APS/RAPS (classification) and CQR (regression); OOD metrics — [ENSTA-U2IS-AI/torch-uncertainty on GitHub](https://github.com/ENSTA-U2IS-AI/torch-uncertainty)
- Evidently: Apache 2.0, Python, ~8.0k stars; 20+ drift tests, classification/regression/ranking metrics, LLM-as-judge checks; Reports, Test Suites (pass/fail for CI) and a monitoring dashboard; calibration metrics not mentioned — [evidentlyai/evidently on GitHub](https://github.com/evidentlyai/evidently)
- NannyML: Apache 2.0, Python, ~2.2k stars; CBPE estimates classification performance without labels, DLE for regression; multivariate (PCA reconstruction) and univariate drift; realized performance once labels arrive — [NannyML/nannyml on GitHub](https://github.com/NannyML/nannyml); CBPE: "if a predicted probability is well calibrated, it can estimate the chance a prediction is wrong" (a 0.9 implies ~90% right), used to estimate confusion-matrix elements and any derived metric; NannyML checks calibration on reference data with 3 stratified splits, fits isotonic regression, compares ECE raw vs calibrated and applies the calibrator only if ECE improves; reference data must not be the training set; assumes no concept drift and no covariate shift into unseen regions — [NannyML performance estimation](https://nannyml.readthedocs.io/en/stable/how_it_works/performance_estimation.html)

### Inferences
- Borrow directly: NannyML's CBPE is the exact mechanism for monitoring a calibrated decision model in production *before* outcomes arrive — the engine can estimate per-question accuracy from the probability stream, then correct the estimate when outcomes land. Its isotonic-recalibration-if-ECE-improves step is a reusable recipe for per-backend, per-question recalibration.
- Borrow: conformal prediction sets (MAPIE/crepes/TorchUncertainty) as the principled form of "abstain or escalate": a `Choice` whose conformal set has more than one member at the target coverage should not be auto-acted on.
- Avoid: assuming vendor "confidence" fields are calibrated; measure ECE/Brier per question type from the ledger.
- Gap: no library connects these metrics to decision-model APIs or to a decision log; the engine would be the first to run reliability diagrams per question/criteria version.

### Gaps
- WhyLabs/whylogs license and features were not confirmed.
- No sources fetched on dedicated calibration-metric libraries (e.g., netcal, uncertainty-calibration); Brier score tooling is covered only implicitly (Clef trains with Brier loss; TorchUncertainty exposes ECE).

## DSPy 3.4.0's Jev integration and the ReAnchor optimizer

### Takeaway
DSPy 3.4.0 (25 Sep 2026) is the closest existing thing to "learning to compound": it makes probabilistic decisions first-class output types (`Noul` with a `threshold`, `Score` with `cuts`, `Choice` with `weights`), decodes probability *evidence* from any generative LM or natively from TypeSafe, and ships ReAnchor, an optimizer that fits those thresholds/cuts/weights against a program-level metric with cross-validated acceptance. It is offline (fit on a trainset, returns a copy of the program), single-program, has no record of production decisions, and does not learn from outcomes over time.

### Cited Findings
- Release 3.4.0 (tagged 25 Sep by isaacbmiller): Jev runs through `lm=` after `pip install dspy[typesafe]` and `TYPESAFE_API_KEY`; three experimental decision types — `Noul` (`.value`, `.probability`, `.confidence`), `Choice[...]` (typed `.value`, option `.probabilities`, `.confidence`), `Score[...]` (continuous `.value`, ordinal `.level`, rubric `.probabilities`, `.confidence`); "thresholds, Score cuts, and Choice weights are applied locally, not sent to the backend"; Jev is "a decision backend, not a general-purpose text generator"; ReAnchor "fits Boolean thresholds, Score cuts, and Choice weights in Predict programs", leaves instructions, criteria and demonstrations unchanged, scores candidates against the whole program's metric, and "a setting must improve the training score and pass a held-out fold check"; `compile()` returns a copy; limitations: "ReAnchor does not guarantee a fixed inference budget", RLM decision outputs unsupported, single-option Choice calibration raises, no automatic generative fallback, no decision streaming, no generative optimizers/fine-tuning with TypeSafe, nested decision outputs lack evidence decoding; "Noul confidence measures distance from its threshold, not statistical calibration. Generative-LM confidence is self-reported" — [DSPy 3.4.0 release](https://github.com/stanfordnlp/dspy/releases/tag/3.4.0); announcement — [Isaac Miller on X](https://x.com/isaacbmiller1/status/2103470217670127720); 3.4.1 fixes a 3.4.0 import crash — [DSPy releases](https://github.com/stanfordnlp/dspy/releases)
- Tutorial: `Urgent = Noul[(True, "..."), (False, "...")]`, `Severity = Score["Cosmetic issue", "Degrades workflow", "Blocks critical path"]`, `Category = Choice[("billing", "..."), ...]`; Noul `threshold` default 0.5 (True when P(True) ≥ threshold); Score value in [0, N−1] with `cuts` (N−1 boundaries) mapping to `.level`; Choice `weights` multiply option probabilities ("higher weights are preferred in close calls"); with generative LMs "DSPy asks the LM for probability evidence, not just a label" and derives the value locally; TypeSafe returns calibrated probabilities directly, requires all outputs to be decision types and supports no temperature; each decision output needs a `desc`/instructions or DSPy errors before calling the LM; `Annotated[bool, Urgent]` returns native values; per-field parameters are set via `predict.fields["urgent"] = {"threshold": 0.7}` and `{"cuts": [0.8, 1.5]}`; criteria via `set_criteria()`; ReAnchor usage `ReAnchor(metric).compile(triage, trainset=trainset)`; algorithm: run program on trainset collecting probability evidence → try candidate parameter values per decision output → 5-fold cross-validation (fewer folds for small sets) → return copy with best parameters; metric is user-supplied `metric(example, prediction, trace=None)`; fitted parameters persist via `save()`/`load()`; reuses cached LM calls so iterating is cheap (`require_cache=False` to allow uncached calls); if defaults already score well `fields` may stay empty; "the page does not explain the exact candidate grid or how fold scores are aggregated" — [DSPy tutorial: Decision-Making with Jev Types](https://dspy.ai/current/tutorials/jev_decisions/); tutorial PR — [stanfordnlp/dspy #10510](https://github.com/stanfordnlp/dspy/pull/10510)
- A downstream project reports running two ReAnchor passes with "mirrored asymmetric metrics" after switching signatures to Noul/Choice (single user report) — [OpenLoops PR #57](https://github.com/urnlahzer/OpenLoops/pull/57)

### Inferences
- Borrow: the three decision types and their *locally applied* parameters (threshold, cuts, weights) as the engine's canonical decision schema; the separation "backend returns probabilities, engine applies policy" is exactly the layering a compounding engine wants, and DSPy has already validated that generative LMs can be coerced into emitting probability evidence so the engine is not Jev-dependent.
- Borrow: ReAnchor's acceptance rule (must improve train score *and* pass a held-out fold) and its program-level metric (optimizing thresholds for the end-to-end outcome, not per-question accuracy) — this is the core of "learning to compound".
- Avoid/extend: ReAnchor is batch and offline. It has no notion of a production decision stream, outcome latency, drift, shadow evaluation, cost budget ("does not guarantee a fixed inference budget") or asymmetric costs beyond what the user encodes in the metric. The engine should run ReAnchor-style refits continuously on ledger data, gate new threshold sets through guarded rollout, and keep the superseded sets for replay.
- Avoid conflating DSPy's `confidence` (distance from threshold) with calibration; the engine should compute calibration from outcomes.

### Gaps
- DSPy's repository license was not visible on the fetched release page.
- ReAnchor's candidate grid, fold aggregation and handling of multi-output interactions are undocumented in the sources fetched; no paper or benchmark for ReAnchor was found.
- The release page shows the day/month (25 Sep) but not the year; 2026 is inferred from the surrounding announcements.

## Gap analysis: what a "compounding decision engine" would provide that does not exist yet

### Takeaway
Every ingredient exists somewhere — probabilistic typed judgments (Jev/Clef/Decider/OpenAI; DSPy types), threshold fitting (ReAnchor, RouteLLM calibrate_threshold, FrugalGPT), gating vocabularies (Guardrails `on_fail`, Claude `allow/deny/ask/defer`, Cedar's algorithm), composition semantics (DMN hit policies), rollout safety (guarded rollouts, apprentice mode), decision records (OPA logs, ADR/MADR/decision journals), durable chains (Temporal/Workflows) and calibration monitoring (NannyML CBPE, conformal libraries). No system combines them, and several specific capabilities exist nowhere.

### Cited Findings
- Vendors delegate composition to user code: TypeSafe says to split judgments and "combine the answers with your own logic" — [TypeSafe docs](https://docs.typesafe.ai/introduction); OpenAI's Decisions API requires "separate requests" for dependent decisions — [Unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/openai-releases-decisions-api-in-public-beta-powered-by-gpt-6-luna/); Clef evaluates questions independently and gives "no confidence thresholds for gating or human review" — [Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/clef-decision-models)
- The only outcome-driven threshold learner for these types (ReAnchor) is offline, single-program and budget-unbounded — [DSPy 3.4.0 release](https://github.com/stanfordnlp/dspy/releases/tag/3.4.0)
- The only shadow-learning mode found for machine decisions (Personalizer apprentice mode) retires 25 August 2026 — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/personalizer/what-is-personalizer); OpenFeature's outcome-join (Tracking) is experimental — [OpenFeature tracking](https://openfeature.dev/specification/sections/tracking)
- Decision-log standards record inputs/results but not probabilities or outcomes (OPA) — [OPA decision logs](https://www.openpolicyagent.org/docs/latest/management-decision-logs/); human templates record probabilities and outcome reviews but are prose — [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/decision-journal/)
- Label-free accuracy estimation from calibrated probabilities exists for tabular models (CBPE) — [NannyML](https://nannyml.readthedocs.io/en/stable/how_it_works/performance_estimation.html) — but no fetched tool applies it to decision-model outputs.
- Leaderboards score decision models on isolated requests where abstention scores zero — [AI Weekly on the Jev Decision Index](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/jev-decision-index-ranks-30-open-weight-decision-models)

### Inferences
What does not exist yet, and what the engine would provide:
1. A vendor-neutral **decision record** that joins question + criteria version, state digest, backend/model version, raw probabilities, applied threshold/policy version, decision mode (live / shadow / escalated / overridden), cost and latency, and a later **outcome** event — OPA-style structure with decision-journal-style probability and outcome fields.
2. **Composition semantics for probabilistic judgments**: DMN hit policies generalized to probabilities (noisy-OR, min, weighted vote, conformal set size) with explicit conflict handling (Camunda-style "incident" when two confident judgments disagree), declared and versioned like Casbin's effect expression.
3. **Chain-level confidence accounting** for the 0.8^n problem: durable workflows track steps but not probabilities; the engine would carry running confidence per chain, decide where to insert redundant/cross-check questions, and budget verification against cost — FrugalGPT's cascade generalized from one query to a chain.
4. **Shadow mode and guarded rollout for threshold sets and backends** (apprentice mode + LaunchDarkly guarded rollouts applied to decisions rather than features), with Temporal-style versioning so in-flight chains finish under their original policy.
5. **Continuous threshold learning** from the ledger — ReAnchor's cross-validated refit run on production outcomes with off-policy estimators (OBP/VW IPW/DR) using logged exploration or override propensities — rather than one-shot offline compilation.
6. **Calibration monitoring per question and backend** (ECE/Brier/reliability diagrams; CBPE-style label-free accuracy estimates; recalibration layers per backend) so vendor "confidence" is never trusted unmeasured.
7. **Abstention/escalation as a first-class outcome** (`ask`/`defer` with a reason, durable human wait), in contrast to leaderboards and policy engines that treat abstention as zero or as deny.
8. **Backend-agnostic judgment interface** over the converging state+questions API (Jev, Clef, Strands Decider, OpenAI Decisions, prompted LLM via DSPy evidence decoding), with per-backend calibration records so backends can be swapped or cascaded.

Licensing posture for dependencies, based on the findings: Apache 2.0/MIT/BSD components are available for every layer (OPA or Cedar for final enforcement; RouteLLM, DSPy types, OBP, VW, MAPIE/crepes/TorchUncertainty, NannyML, Evidently, NeMo Guardrails, Guardrails AI, DeepEval/Ragas/autoevals, Temporal); avoid building the core on Camunda 8 (Camunda License 1.0), Phoenix (ELv2), Inngest server (SSPL) or Unleash (AGPL) without a licensing review.

### Gaps
- No academic or industry source was found that names or addresses "compounding" calibrated decisions across chains; the 0.8^n framing appears to be the brief's own.
- Whether any closed DI platform (FICO, Pegasystems, SAS) already implements per-decision calibration monitoring could not be determined from public abstracts.
- Independent calibration evaluations of Jev, Clef, Strands Decider or the OpenAI Decisions API were not found; all calibration claims are vendor claims as of 15 October 2026.

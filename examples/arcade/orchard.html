    How this world works

    Each scout takes one noisy sugar reading and states an honest probability that the fruit is ripe; alone, a scout is right about four times in five. Scouts of one family carry the same sensor, so most of their error is shared: their mistakes correlate at 0.7. Families are independent of each other.

    Both arms ask the same scouts about the same fruit through a real ensemble gate. The first pools them as independent. The second passes the engine a correlation for each pair, which shrinks the pooled evidence to what is independent; with the true value the pool's stated confidence matches what it delivers.

    What to look for: more scouts of one family buy almost nothing, a second family buys a lot, and assuming too little correlation picks more fruit while quietly breaking the promise.

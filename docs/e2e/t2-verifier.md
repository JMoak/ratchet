# T2: the independent verifier and the chain ratchet

Wave 1, track T2 of the end-to-end evaluation. The claims under test are decision point #2 in
[vision.md](../vision.md): an independent judge over candidate outputs turns a fragile chain into a
ratchet; per-step reliability becomes `q = p(1-f) / (p(1-f) + (1-p)(1-t))` ("a 90% verifier moves
0.80 to 0.97"); and decomposing a check into sub-questions pooled in log-odds beats one question
and beats thresholding each and AND-ing.

Run: `experiments/out/e2e/t2-verifier/2026-11-12T10-32-34-553Z-live` (gitignored), git HEAD
`c9155b9`, model `typesafe/jev-1.14` through OpenRouter (recorded as `typesafe/jev-1.14-20261014`
on all 19,756 ledger rows), 5,164 billed requests, **$0.1526** ledger-recorded spend (plus about
$0.0002 for a three-item smoke test), no failed-open row. Code: `experiments/e2e/t2-verifier/`.
The offline mode (`--offline`, a seeded `SimOracle`) runs the same wiring at no cost.

## Headline

- **Jev 1.14 is a very strong verifier on this corpus, and the engine reads it correctly.** On 432
  held-out candidates from task families never seen in fitting, AUC 0.998 [0.996, 1.000]; at the
  bar frozen on the fit split (act at p(pass) >= 0.48) it committed 135 outputs, all correct
  (precision 100%, exact lower 97.3%), and held back all 270 wrong ones, every one of nine typed
  error kinds at 100%. Challenge split: 119 of 120 errors held back, including 30 of 30 that carried
  a planted "approve this" note. Over all splits, 7 of 600 wrong candidates passed; 5 of them were
  SQL exclusive upper bounds that drop the last day of a period.
- **Its probabilities are underconfident, not miscalibrated in the dangerous direction.** One-sided
  ECE of p(pass) 0.082 on held-out; candidates scored 0.2 to 0.5 passed 23 of 27 times; 88 of 88
  above 0.9 passed. The cost is false alarms: 16.7% of correct outputs held back at the 0.48 bar,
  45.7% at 0.9, concentrated in two policy-email families (24/27 and 12/12 correct replies held
  back there, at most 3/27 elsewhere).
- **The ratchet formula predicts the accuracy of what is committed, and nothing else.** Committed
  steps were correct 1,132 of 1,132 times, as q = 1.000 predicts (a weak test: t was 100%). End-to-end
  success was far below the formula's: 48% and 53% observed against 74% and 88% predicted at
  p = 0.8 and 0.9; with six attempts 62% and 58% against 96.9% and 97.3% (revised 2026-11-12:
  the arm tested three distinct candidates, not six; see section 3); at the pre-declared strict
  bar 0.9, 0 of 200 chains finished against 20% and 32% predicted. False alarms are not independent
  across retries: after one correct candidate was held back, the next correct one in the same step
  was held back 55.9% of the time against a 15.0% base rate.
- **Verification still paid at p = 0.8** (48% of chains truly finished against 16% unverified, and
  every chain that claimed success was right, 101/101), was inconclusive at p = 0.9 (53% vs 43%),
  and was worse than no verification at the strict bar (0% vs 16% and 43%).
- **Decomposition plus log-odds did not beat one holistic question.** Eight conjunct members pooled
  in log-odds: AUC 0.992 against 0.998 (paired difference -0.006 [-0.010, -0.002]), ECE 0.198 against
  0.082, with 37% of verdicts saturated beyond 1e-6 of 0 or 1 and a fitted bar of 0.99999991. It
  did beat thresholding each member at 0.5 and AND-ing (AUC +0.107 [0.072, 0.144]). The learned
  combiner (`fitLogistic`) tied the holistic question on ranking and was the best calibrated and
  best transferring of all (ECE 0.036; 35% coverage at 99.3% precision with 7.4% false alarms).
- **`runChain` composes badly with gates.** Its `verify` returns a bare `Decision` and it re-rules
  with its own policy, so a gate's floors, one-way doors, shadow mode and blind audits are dropped:
  a repro commits a `DROP TABLE` step the gate said to review, in 4 of 4 configurations. Live, all
  2,220 chain rows say `shadow/review` while the chains committed 1,132 steps.
- **Battery ledger readers cannot read decompositions.** `memberCorrelation` finds 0 rows on a
  battery ledger, and `Engine.label` scores every member against the battery's truth, so a perfect
  member of a conjunctive battery reports 75% accuracy.

## 3. The ratchet

100 chains of 8 steps per base rate. Each step is a fresh instance of a held-out family (cycling
through all six), so the frozen t and f apply. Attempts in a step never repeat bytes: correct
attempts cycle the three correct styles, wrong ones distinct error types. "Predicted" uses held-out
t and f at the bar; "from realised" plugs in the t and f the chains themselves produced.

| quantity | predicted | range (Wilson t, f) | from realised t, f | observed |

Revised 2026-11-12 (foundations review,
[verified_chains.md](../../research_notes/Foundations%20review%202026-11/verified_chains.md)):
the six-attempt arm replayed cached verdicts for identical candidates (the k-th and (k+3)-th
correct candidates of a step had the same variant, state hash and score in all 165 pairs), so it
tested three distinct candidates per step, not six. The two six-attempt rows read 0.989 and
0.998, independence over six fresh draws, as their range column still does. With three distinct
correct styles, independence gives 0.969 and 0.973 (at the chains' 15.0% false-alarm base rate);
the frailty model fitted to the two rates below gives 0.612 and 0.615.

Every chain that claimed success was truly correct (48/48, 53/53, 62/62, 58/58). The gap is
completion, and the post-hoc analysis locates it:

- **Exhaustion is concentrated where false alarms are.** At the 0.48 bar, warranty steps exhausted
  40.2% (47/117) and 37.6% (47/125) of the time; every other family 0% to 2.3%. Using each family's
  own held-out f instead of the pooled one predicts 0.169 and 0.202 for chain completion, below the
  observed 48% and 53%: per-instance variation sits between the pooled and per-family views.
- **Retries are correlated.** When a step's first correct candidate was held back, its next correct
  candidate was held back 55.9% [48.4, 63.1] of the time (base 15.0%); at the strict bar 79.4%
  (base 54.5%). The formula's completion term assumes independent attempts; a near-deterministic
  verifier that dislikes an instance dislikes every fresh correct answer to it.
- **Rows from chains are a selected sample.** Realised false alarm inside the chains was 21.0% and
  23.0% at 0.48 (held-out 16.7%) and 65.5% and 67.0% at 0.9 (held-out 45.7%), because a retry row
  exists only when the attempt before it was held back.

**Semantics.** Zero stop/commit violations over 830 chain runs (verified, unverified, 6-attempt,
strict, refuse): every committed step had exactly one `act` ruling and it was the last attempt;
checkpoints matched commits; the final state never held an unverified proposal; no chain continued
past a failed step; attempts never exceeded the budget. With `refuseBelow: 0.2`, 27 of 30 chains
stopped with `refused` at the first confidently rejected proposal (completion 6.7% against 43.3%
for the same chains and verdicts without it). Non-finite `maxAttempts` is not bounded (finding
T2-09).

**What reaches the ledger.** 2,220 rows for 2,220 gate asks. They carry chain, step and attempt only
because the harness passed tags. All 2,220 say `mode: shadow, action: review`; the chains committed
1,132 steps on them. The chain gate's own report reads all 2,220 as unselected shadow evidence and
fits a bar of 0.870 (the held-out bar was 0.48), because retry rows over-represent hard steps.
Learning can use these rows for calibration only if it can tell first attempts from retries, which
nothing on a row records.

## 5. Findings

Severity, category, evidence, proposed fix. Repros live in `experiments/e2e/t2-verifier/repro/`,
run with `bun <path>`; each exits 1 while the bug exists.

**T2-01. High. Engine bug / design gap. `runChain` drops a gate's floors, one-way doors, mode and
audits.** `Step.verify` returns a `Decision`, and `runChain` rules it afresh with its own policy
(`src/compound/chain.ts:71`), so the gate's ruling and action are discarded. Because a step's
verifications reach the ledger only through a gate (docs/roadmap.md), the documented way to keep
evidence is the way that loses the controls. Repro `chain-drops-gate-controls.ts`: a review floor
(policy with `refuseBelow`), a `oneWayDoor` floor under `policyFromCosts(1, 1)` (floored neutral
0.5 meets actAbove 0.5), a gate left in shadow, and an enforce gate with `auditRate: 1` each commit a
`DROP TABLE` step the gate said to review (4/4); the ledger records `review`. Live: 2,220 rows
`shadow/review`, 1,132 steps committed. Fix: let `verify` return the gate's `AskResult` (or give
`runChain` a `Gate`/`Asker`) and commit on `result.action === "act"`; in shadow, follow the
caller's baseline; refuse when the gate refuses. Document it in `docs/architecture.md`.

**T2-02. High. Docs mismatch / design gap. The ratchet formula is presented as chain reliability;
it is only the precision of commits.** `docs/architecture.md` ("Per-step reliability becomes q")
and the `runChain` comment ("end-to-end reliability is set by the verifier and the retry budget,
not by n") predict 74% to 99.8% completion where 48% to 62% happened, and 20% to 32% where 0%
happened. Completion depends on the false-alarm rate per item and its correlation across retries
(55.9% vs 15.0% here), neither of which the formula or `sim/` models. Fix: state q as commit
precision; give completion as `(1 - (1 - a)^m)^n` with a warning that it assumes independent
attempts; add a correlated-false-alarm row to the chain table in `sim/main.ts`; recommend measuring
"held back again after a held-back correct attempt" from the ledger (needs T2-03).

**T2-03. Medium. Design gap. Chain traces are not persisted, and chain rows look unselected.**
Known gap, now quantified: rows carry no chain, step, attempt or commit unless the caller tags
them; every row reads as an unselected shadow row although the chain acted on it, and retries
exist only because an earlier attempt was held back. The chain gate's report fits 0.870 where
held-out supports 0.48; realised f in chains 21% to 67% vs 17% to 46% held-out. Fix
(`src/compound/chain.ts`, `src/ledger/calibration.ts`): write chain, step and attempt into each
verify row and a committed flag (or a chain summary row); have `isUnselected` exclude attempts
after the first, or tag them `retry`.

**T2-04. Medium. Design gap / docs mismatch. `report().fitted` for a judge gate is two-sided and is
used as `actAbove`.** `fitThreshold` reads max(p, 1 - p) (`src/engine.ts:439`), so confident "no"
answers count toward the promise; `examples/tool-gate.ts:157` and `docs/cli.md` use the result as
the act bar on p(yes). Repro `report-bar-two-sided.ts`: reported bar 0.9 promising 95%, acting at it
gives 80% precision. Live it erred the other way: the bar was 0.5 (coverage 100%, no review band),
its own promise was 93.8% on held-out (below the 95% target) while acts were 100% precise. Either
way the number reported is not the number acted on. Acceptance (`src/acceptance.ts:280`) is already
one-sided. Fix: fit judge gates' bars on the go probability against `actWasRight` (with
`inferGoIsYes`), report the two-sided figure as leading-answer accuracy, and update the example and
CLI docs.

**T2-05. Medium. Docs mismatch. Log-odds is the wrong default for a conjunctive battery.** The
vision and architecture say to combine decomposed sub-questions in log-odds; `GateSpec.combine`
defaults to it. On an 8-member conjunctive battery: lower AUC than one question, ECE 0.198, 37% of
verdicts saturated, fitted bar 0.99999991, frozen coverage 25% with 34% false alarms. The measured
correlation shrink does not repair it (ECE 0.210). `fitLogistic` fixed it (ECE 0.036, best frozen
operating point). Fix: in `src/gate.ts` and `docs/architecture.md`, recommend log-odds only for
redundant members (several framings of one question) and `min` or a learned combiner for
conjuncts; warn in `report()` when a battery's pooled verdicts saturate.

**T2-06. Medium. Engine bug + design gap. Battery members cannot be read from the ledger.**
`memberCorrelation` reads `members` on a row, which battery combined rows never carry
(`src/ledger/calibration.ts:464`), so it returns 0 rows for any battery, while `src/gate.ts` says to
measure a battery's correlation from the ledger. `inheritOutcome` gives members the battery's truth
(`src/ledger/ledger.ts:175`), right for redundant members and wrong for conjuncts: repro
`battery-ledger-readers.ts` shows a perfect member reported at 0.75; live, the dates member reads
89.6% against its own question and 53.5% inherited. Fix: pair battery member rows by `requestId`
and `questionId` in `memberCorrelation`; let `Engine.label` accept per-member truths and record
whether a battery is redundant or conjunctive; document what inherited member accuracy means.

**T2-07. Medium. Jev limitation / docs mismatch. Run-to-run drift is larger than documented for
jev-1.14.** The vision says Jev is near-deterministic (spread about 0.01, zero flips on 40
repeats, measured on jev-1.13); `policy.ts` cites 0.08 to 0.12. Here 11.4% of 924 repeated
holistic requests moved more than 0.12 (max 0.51) and 5.5% flipped lean; battery members flipped
2.6% to 2.9%. `policyFromProfile`'s default half-width of 0.05 is narrower than this. Fix: re-measure
per model pin in `docs/live-findings.md`, cite it in `vision.md` and `policy.ts`, and size bands
from measured repeat spread.

**T2-08. Low. Jev limitation. Two blind spots and two false-alarm clusters.** SQL exclusive upper
bounds that drop the last day passed 5/5 (fit, p 0.72 to 0.91). Policy-email replies were held back
in bulk in two of three families (warranty 24/27, rail change 12/12), mostly on `complete` and
`supported`; 15 warranty items are arguably ambiguous (post-hoc flag, `corrections.json`), the rail
replies are not. No engine change; per-family bars or per-family evidence grades would expose it.

**T2-09. Low. Engine bug. `runChain` accepts non-finite attempt budgets.** `Math.max(1,
step.maxAttempts ?? 3)` (`src/compound/chain.ts:63`) passes `Infinity` (retries without end; repro
stops it after 10,000 proposals) and `NaN` (zero attempts, reported `exhausted`). Repro
`chain-attempt-bounds.ts`. Fix: require a finite integer of at least 1, else throw.

**T2-10. Low. Ergonomics / docs. A verifier policy with `refuseBelow` ends the whole chain on the
first confidently wrong proposal.** Documented in architecture, but `policyWithBand(..., { refuse:
true })` is the documented way to build a band and nothing warns: completion fell from 43.3% to
6.7% (27/30 chains refused). Fix: say in `chain.ts` and `docs/architecture.md` that a step
verifier's policy should not refuse, and use `ChainOptions.floor` for stop conditions.

**T2-11. Low. Ergonomics. Labelling a `JsonlLedger` costs a full reread per label.** `label`
calls `all()`; `Engine.label` adds `get` and `request`; a battery writes one outcome per member.
Census of 432 battery requests (3,888 rows) took 125 s against 3.7 s for 432 single rows; repro
`jsonl-label-cost.ts`: 87, 134 and 213 ms per label at 100, 200 and 400 battery requests. Fix: index
ids on first read in `src/ledger/ledger.ts`, and label a battery's members in one append.

**Confirmations.** Blind census through `ratchet review` labelled all 5,164 decision rows with 0
skips and 0 mismatches. JSONL appends under 8-way concurrency kept exact row counts. A battery's
combined row equals the offline pool of its members exactly. The learned combiner runs through a
gate as documented. Digesting numbers in code helped as claimed. Planted approval notes in the
judged output moved nothing (30/30 held back).

## 6. Limits

- The corpus is authored. Candidates are scripted mutations of templates, not model outputs, so
  error realism is limited and items within an instance are correlated (intervals on AUC and
  coverage are cluster bootstraps over instances, but families number six per split). Jev may find
  injected, single-edit errors easier than the errors a real proposer makes.
- t = 100% on held-out makes q = 1 trivially; the q formula was tested in a regime where it cannot
  fail, and checked offline only with a simulated verifier.
- The chain is a pipeline of independent tasks: a wrong committed step does not corrupt later
  steps' inputs, and the proposer's correct styles are finite (three per step), so a fourth correct
  retry repeats bytes. Both shape the 6-attempt result.
- The strict operating point was declared after the fit freeze, not with it; held-out verdicts were
  on disk but unread when it was declared.
- One model (jev-1.14). One decider (the author); the warranty ambiguity shows the labels carry
  judgement, and the coordinator's relabel of `spotcheck.json` is the check on that.
- Sub-truths for battery members are this evaluation's construction (`BREAKS` in `types.ts`); the
  member tables depend on them, the gate results do not.

Files: `experiments/e2e/t2-verifier/` (runner `main.ts`, corpus generator `corpus.ts` and
`domains/`, `labels.json`, `spotcheck.json`, `corrections.json`, `analysis.ts`, `posthoc.ts`,
`repro/`). Raw outputs, ledgers, `results.md` and `posthoc.md` are in the run directory above.

## Restrictions that shape how Ratchet may be used

**Embed it; do not resell access.** The MCA lets you include the API in your own software
applications (§2.2) but not offer the Services "as a standalone service" (§2.3(a)).
OpenRouter forbids "reselling API access to Models or otherwise developing a competing
service" (§7.4) and transferring your access (§7.14). A library each user runs with their
own key fits. A hosted endpoint that forwards other people's questions to a model on your
key does not. A hosted application with its own purpose that makes model calls for its
users, such as a live arcade, is an application you answer for; decide that deliberately.
The offline arcade makes no model calls.

**Do not distill from the ledger.** The MCA forbids using the Services or any Output to
perform model distillation, train a model to imitate the output of the Services, or develop
a similar or competing product (§2.3(b)). Ledger rows store model Output: probabilities,
choices and scores. Ratchet's fitting (thresholds, isotonic recalibration, learned
composition) uses your outcome labels as the target and produces a few parameters, not a
model of the service's answers. That is our reading, not a ruling; get advice before
building on it at scale. Never train a model to reproduce a provider's verdicts from
exported ledgers. Outcome labels you collect yourself are not the provider's Output.

**Treat pricing as the provider's information.** The MCA lists "all pricing information"
among TypeSafe's Confidential Information (§14.1). If you hold a direct agreement, keep
negotiated or non-public prices out of anything you publish.

**Names and marks.** The MCA grants neither party the right to use the other's name, brand
or logo, or to announce the agreement publicly (§16.4). Name a provider only to say what you
call; do not use its logos, imply endorsement, or put its product name in your package or
service name.

**Say when a model was involved.** TypeSafe's AUP prohibits misleading others about whether
content, including Output, was generated by AI (§1.4). The arcade labels live play; do the
same wherever a model's judgment shapes something a person sees.

## What leaves your machine

Every `ask` sends the rendered state and the question text to the backend. Floors, policy
and the ledger stay local. Under the MCA, TypeSafe may use Customer Data in perpetuity to
derive Telemetry, and does not train models on it without your consent (§4.1); Telemetry
(logs, hashes, summary statistics, classifications and metrics) is usable without
restriction (§4.3). OpenRouter takes a license to the content you send in order to operate
its service (§6.1). Keep secrets, credentials and personal data you may not share out of the
state; compute and redact in code before you build it.

## Decisions about people

The MCA states that the Services "may produce inaccurate or erroneous output" and that you
are responsible for independently evaluating it (§9.3). TypeSafe's AUP has no clause on
high-risk uses; that is not permission. Do not use Ratchet as the sole basis for decisions
with legal or similarly significant effects on people, such as employment, credit, housing,
insurance, healthcare, education or legal matters. Keep a human reviewer in the loop, and
check the law that applies to you, for example GDPR Article 22 on solely automated decisions
and the EU AI Act's human-oversight requirements for high-risk systems.

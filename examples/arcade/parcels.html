    How this world works

    A parcel is bad when it carries a stop stamp, weighs over the belt's 8 kg, has an unverified address, or is damaged. The first three are on the depot's manifest, which is trusted. Damage shows only on a noisy scanner. The sender's note is untrusted text.

    The judge is a seeded toy that weighs the scanner and the manifest and is, on purpose, persuadable: every claim of approval in a note moves it toward shipping. The engine's floors read the manifest's own values through the state's entries, so a sentence in a note cannot trip or clear one, and a floor only ever raises caution.

    What to look for: rules remove every bad parcel a rule covers, whatever the note says, and leave damage, which no rule covers. That last part is the judge's, and it depends on whether the judge is shown the note at all.

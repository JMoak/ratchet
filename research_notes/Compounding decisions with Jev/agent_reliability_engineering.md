# Agent reliability engineering: turning imperfect per-step decisions into reliable end-to-end outcomes (2024 to Oct 2026)

Scope note: notes compiled 2026-10-15. "Vendor claim" marks numbers reported by the company selling the thing; "independent" marks third-party measurement; "self-reported" marks a user or plugin author measuring their own system. Jev (TypeSafe AI) launched 2026-09-15, so every Jev number is at most one month old and none has an independent replication.

## Key question 1: Reliability measurement — how per-step error compounds, and how it is measured (pass^k, SWE-bench variance, METR horizons, MAST, drift)

### Takeaway
Single-run pass rates overstate agent reliability by a wide margin: the same agent run k times succeeds every time far less often (tau-bench pass^8 < 25% vs pass^1 ~61%; Thinkingbox 65% once vs 25% all 20 runs), and two 2026 studies find per-step reliability stays "well below 1" even for frontier models, so success decays geometrically with step count — with the twist that the decay is driven by step count and by the model conditioning on its own earlier errors, not by context length alone.

### Cited Findings
- tau-bench introduced pass^k ("probability that an agent succeeds on every one of k independent runs of the same task", averaged over tasks). State-of-the-art function-calling agents such as gpt-4o "succeed on <50% of the tasks" and are "quite inconsistent (pass^8 <25% in retail)". Yao, Shinn, Razavi, Narasimhan; submitted 2024-06-17. — [arXiv 2406.12045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045)
- Secondary reading of the tau-bench body: GPT-4o pass^1 ~61% retail and ~35% airline; a reviewer notes pass^k variance mixes agent inconsistency with user-simulator inconsistency, which the paper does not separate; τ²-bench (arXiv 2506.07982) is the dual-control follow-up. — [themoonlight review](https://themoonlight.io/fr/review/-bench-a-benchmark-for-tool-agent-user-interaction-in-real-world-domains); [beancount research log](https://beancount.io/nl/bean-labs/research-logs/2026/06/12/tau-bench-tool-agent-user-interaction-real-world-domains)
- Anthropic's eval guide (2026-01-09) defines the harness ("the system that lets a model act as an agent... evaluating 'an agent' means evaluating the harness and the model together"), recommends "pass@k for tools where one success matters, pass^k for agents where consistency is essential", and gives the arithmetic: a 75% per-trial agent passes 3/3 trials about 42% of the time (0.75^3). It also reports SWE-bench Verified rising from ~40% to >80% in a year and Opus 4.5 going from 42% to 95% on CORE-Bench after grading/task fixes (i.e., grader error can dwarf model error). — [Anthropic, Demystifying evals for AI agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)
- "On Randomness in Agentic Evals" (Bjarnason, Silva, Monperrus; v1 2026-02-06, v3 2026-03-25, ICLR 2026): 60,000 SWE-bench Verified trajectories across 3 models and 2 scaffolds; single-run pass@1 estimates shift 2.2 to 6.0 percentage points depending on which run is picked; standard deviation stays above 1.5 points even at temperature 0; trajectories diverge "within the first few percent of tokens"; recommends reporting pass^k (pessimistic) and pass@k (optimistic) with k > 1 and sizing run counts by power analysis. — [arXiv 2602.07150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07150)
- Independent re-run (LayerLens, 2026-08-28): Qwen3.7 Max under mini-swe-agent on SWE-bench Verified scored 70.6% and 72.8% in two identical runs; 53 of 500 tasks (10.6%) flipped verdict (21 pass-to-fail, 32 fail-to-pass), so the "headline" could be anywhere from 66.4% (passed both) to 77% (passed either). Same post: Microsoft's Thinkingbox — strongest model passes 65.36% of 507 business-workflow tasks once but only 25.25% of tasks all 20 times; only 16% of 445 reviewed LLM benchmarks ran any statistical test. — [LayerLens](https://layerlens.ai/blog/the-test-your-agent-never-took)
- WebArena no-memory baseline: pass@3 to pass^3 gap "frequently exceeds 20%". — [arXiv 2608.18066 (On the Fragility of Self-Improving Agents)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.18066)
- METR, "Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Software Tasks" (Kwa, West, Becker et al.; v1 2025-03-18; NeurIPS 2025): 50% time horizon "doubled every 207 days with a 95% bootstrapped confidence interval 166-240 days"; "models' 80% time horizons are 4-6x shorter" than 50% horizons; 80% horizon doubling time 204 days (similar); best 50% horizon at publication o3 at 110 min; success modeled as p = σ((log h_agent − log t_task)·β_agent). — [arXiv 2503.14499](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14499)
- METR Time Horizon 1.1 (2026-01-29): task suite grew 170 → 228 (73 HCAST tasks added, 15 removed, 53 updated); 8h+ tasks 14 → 31 but only 5 of 31 have measured human baselines; post-2023 doubling time 130.8 days [107, 161] (TH1.0: 165.3 [129, 211]); since-2024 doubling 88.6 days; highest TH1.1 50% estimate Claude Opus 4.5 at 320 min [170, 729], GPT-5 214 min [117, 480]; scaffold matters ("GPT-4o and o3 scored significantly higher under Vivaria than under Inspect"). — [METR TH1.1 release](https://metr.org/blog/2026-1-29-time-horizon-1-1/)
- METR dashboard: "Measurements above 16 hrs are unreliable with our current task suite" (2026-05-08 update); page "no longer actively updated" as of 2026-09-08; illustrative per-task consistency: on tasks taking humans 90 min to 3 h, a GPT-5 agent (horizon ~2 h 17 min) "succeeds 100% of the time for around one-third of the tasks", fails 100% on about a third, and is mixed on the rest. — [metr.org/time-horizons](https://metr.org/time-horizons/)
- Secondhand only: METR's May 2026 Frontier Risk Report reportedly put the public frontier near 12 h at 50% (forecast-market comment); a LessWrong-style analysis argues 80% horizons are sensitive to human-baseline estimation and may be "an order of magnitude off". Treat both as unverified. — [Manifold comment](https://manifold.markets/Bayesian/best-metr-time-horizons-in-2026); [greaterwrong](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/sBEzomgnYJmYHki9T/updated-metr-s-data-can-t-distinguish-between-trajectories)
- MAST (Cemri et al., UC Berkeley; arXiv 2503.13657; NeurIPS 2025 D&B): 14 failure modes in 3 categories across 7 frameworks and 200+ traces (150+ human-annotated, Cohen's κ 0.88). Shares of failures: FC1 Specification 41.77% (step repetition 17.14%, disobey task spec 10.98%, unaware of termination conditions 9.82%, loss of conversation history 3.33%, disobey role 0.5%); FC2 Inter-agent misalignment 36.94% (reasoning-action mismatch 13.98%, fail to ask for clarification 11.65%, task derailment 7.15%, conversation reset 2.33%, information withholding 1.66%, ignored input 0.17%); FC3 Task verification 21.30% (premature termination 7.82%, no/incomplete verification 6.82%, incorrect verification 6.66%). An o1-based LLM annotator reached accuracy 0.94 / F1 0.80 / κ 0.77 few-shot (0.89 / 0.64 / 0.58 zero-shot). Interventions on ChatDev: improved role specification +9.4% success; adding a high-level verification step +15.6% absolute. ChatDev only 33.33% correct on ProgramDev. — [arXiv 2503.13657](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v2)
- "The Illusion of Diminishing Returns" (Sinha, Arun, Goel, Staab, Geiping; v1 2025-09-11, ICLR 2026): with constant independent step accuracy p and no self-correction, horizon at success rate s is H_s(p) = ln(s)/ln(p) (so 50% horizon ≈ 3 steps at p=0.8, ≈69 at p=0.99, ≈693 at p=0.999). Key empirical findings: per-step accuracy falls as steps accumulate even when the plan and knowledge are given; "models become more likely to make mistakes when the context contains their errors from prior turns" (self-conditioning), and scaling model size does not remove it; "CoT does not mitigate the self-conditioning effect" (Appendix I); thinking does — steps executable in a single turn at ≥80% accuracy: GPT-5 (thinking) 2176, Claude-4 Sonnet 432, Grok 4 384, Gemini 2.5 Pro 120, DeepSeek-R1 >100, non-thinking models (DeepSeek-V3, Kimi K2) fail beyond ~6; even the best small open model (Qwen3-32B) drops below 50% turn accuracy within 15 turns despite near-perfect first-step accuracy. — [arXiv 2509.09677](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09677)
- "How Fast Do Agents Rot?" (Mittal, single author; arXiv 2609.01660; 2026-08-31): 9 models (6 open 1.2B–671B, 3 proprietary), 4 task families, 5 horizons, 3 context regimes. Success "follows a geometric law controlled by one per-step reliability parameter" that "rises with model scale but stays well below 1"; on the agentic tool-use task every model "fell from near-perfect success to near zero within sixteen steps" (n=10,664 trajectories); "step count, not context length, drives decay": bounding the context window made decay steeper (logit slope −0.69 vs −0.44, p=3×10⁻⁶); projected reliability ~0.42 at GAIA-length horizons and ~0.24 at 100-step production horizons. Caveat: single-author preprint filed under physics.soc-ph. — [arXiv 2609.01660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01660)
- "The Horizon Gap" survey (Chen, Wang, Qu; arXiv 2608.06663; 2026-08-07; 1,547 papers): outcome-only signals "grow uninformative as horizons lengthen"; open problems include "separating model capability from harness capability" and "correlated bias in process-level signals used for both training and evaluation". — [arXiv 2608.06663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06663)
- "Engineering Reliable Coding Agents" (Jarmak; arXiv 2608.13867; 2026-08-14): "many apparent model failures originate elsewhere in the system"; failure-source taxonomy: harness, execution state, retrieval, memory/state management, permissions, review interfaces, resource allocation; 193 gated practices and 206 reliability records compiled. — [arXiv 2608.13867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13867)
- SABER (Cuadron, Yu, Liu, Gupta; arXiv 2512.07850; 2025-11-26): on τ-bench, "each additional deviation in a mutating action reduced the odds of success by up to 92% on Airline and up to 96% on Retail" while non-mutating deviations had "little to no effect"; errors grow with context length as agents "drift from their role and act on stale constraints". — [arXiv 2512.07850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07850)
- "Agent Drift" (arXiv 2601.04170; Jan 2026; single independent author) defines semantic, coordination and behavioral drift; its headline "42% reduction in task success" and "3.2x increase in human intervention" are simulated projections, not deployment measurements. — [arXiv 2601.04170](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04170v1)
- Anthropic multi-agent research system (2025-06-13): on BrowseComp, token usage alone explained 80% of performance variance; token usage + tool calls + model choice explained 95%; agents use ~4x chat tokens, multi-agent ~15x. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)

### Inferences
- The two 2026 compounding studies converge on an actionable model: treat the agent as having a per-step reliability p that is high but < 1, and budget horizon as ln(s)/ln(p). Doubling the achievable horizon at fixed s requires roughly halving (1−p). This is the quantitative case for inserting cheap per-step checks: each check that catches a fraction of errors raises effective p.
- Self-conditioning (Illusion paper) plus "mutating actions dominate failure" (SABER) imply that the two highest-value interventions are (a) preventing errors in state-mutating steps specifically and (b) keeping error traces out of the context the model conditions on — which is a context-management decision, not just a safety one.
- Mittal's finding that bounding context steepened decay is a warning against blunt truncation as a reliability fix; relevance-scored compaction (keep what matters) is the version that is consistent with both papers.
- The 4–6x gap between 50% and 80% horizons (METR) is a direct measurement of how far "sometimes works" is from "reliably works": a harness that lifts per-task consistency buys more usable horizon than a model upgrade that lifts pass@1.
- Eval noise (SD >1.5 points at T=0; 10.6% verdict flips) means harness A/B tests need multiple runs per task; 2–3 point deltas are within noise.

### Gaps
- No official METR 2026 statement of the current 50%/80% horizon ratio; TH1.1 release reports only 50% values.
- MAST per-system failure rates (Figure 1) and Table 4 AG2 intervention numbers were not in the retrieved text.
- The Illusion paper's self-conditioning magnitude is only in a figure (turn-100 accuracy vs injected error rate); no numeric drop was extractable.
- No independent replication of Mittal's "near zero within sixteen steps" result was found.
- Agent "drift" has no empirical quantification beyond simulation.

## Key question 2: Harness patterns and their evidence (Anthropic patterns and harness posts; Claude Code hooks and auto mode; OpenAI Agents SDK guardrails; LangChain/LangGraph middleware; Vercel AI SDK experimental_evaluate; Cloudflare Agents; Google ADK; Microsoft Agent Framework)

### Takeaway
Every major harness now exposes the same four interception points — before a tool runs, after a tool returns, before/after a model call, and at stop/compaction — and most ship a human-approval primitive; only Claude Code's auto mode has published measured gate accuracy (0.4% false-positive, 17% miss rate on real overeager actions), and the LangChain, Vercel and eve integrations slot Jev into exactly those interception points with thresholds but no published measurements.

### Cited Findings
- Anthropic "Building effective agents" (2024-12-19): distinguishes workflows ("predefined code paths") from agents (model "dynamically direct[s] their own processes"); five workflow patterns — prompt chaining with programmatic "gates" between steps, routing (classify then dispatch), parallelization (sectioning and voting), orchestrator-workers, evaluator-optimizer — plus guidance that agents should "gain 'ground truth' from the environment at each step", pause at human checkpoints, be tested in sandboxes with guardrails, and have stopping conditions such as a maximum number of iterations because autonomy raises "the risk of compounding errors". — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)
- Anthropic "Effective harnesses for long-running agents" (Justin Young, 2025-11-26): failure modes observed — one-shotting (ran out of context mid-implementation), premature completion, unclean handoffs, marking features complete without end-to-end testing, setup overhead. Harness: initializer agent writes init.sh, claude-progress.txt, an initial git commit and a feature_list.json where each feature has test steps and a `passes` field (JSON chosen "because models are less likely to inappropriately overwrite it than Markdown"; coding agents may only change `passes`); descriptive git commits "let agents revert bad changes"; unit tests and curl were insufficient — Claude "failed to recognize when features didn't work end-to-end until it was explicitly prompted to use browser automation" (Puppeteer MCP). No quantitative results; >200 features in the claude.ai-clone example; Opus 4.5 looping in the Agent SDK "fell short" of a production-quality app from a prompt alone. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents)
- Anthropic "Harness design for long-running application development" (Prithvi Rajasekaran, 2026-03-24): planner / generator / evaluator agents with "sprint contracts" (generator proposes what "done" means and how to verify; evaluator agrees before work starts); evaluator drives the live app via Playwright MCP, each criterion has a hard threshold and failing any one fails the sprint; failure modes — context degradation, "context anxiety" (Sonnet 4.5 wrapped up early near perceived context limit; Opus 4.5 "largely removed" it so resets were dropped), self-evaluation bias ("agents tended to praise their own work"), lenient QA ("identified real issues, then talked themselves into approving"). Measured: retro-game-maker V1 (Opus 4.5) solo run 20 min / $9 with core mode broken vs full harness 6 h / $200 playable (">20x more expensive"); DAW V2 (Opus 4.6) 3 h 50 min / $124.70; frontend experiments 5–15 iterations, up to ~4 h, scores "improved over iterations before plateauing". Separate evaluator called "a strong lever"; "tuning a standalone evaluator to be skeptical was found to be far more tractable than making a generator critical of its own work"; evaluator "worth its cost when a task sits beyond what the model reliably does alone, and is unnecessary overhead within that boundary". — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps); [InfoQ summary 2026-04-04](https://infoq.com/news/2026/04/anthropic-three-agent-harness-ai/)
- Anthropic managed-agents post: harnesses "encode assumptions about what Claude can't do on its own" that "go stale as models improve" (context-reset workaround needed for Sonnet 4.5, not Opus 4.5). — [Anthropic (via search summary)](https://anthropic.com/engineering/managed-agents)
- Anthropic multi-agent research system (2025-06-13): multi-agent (Opus 4 lead, Sonnet 4 subagents) beat single Opus 4 by 90.2% on internal eval; parallelization cut research time "up to 90%"; rewriting tool descriptions cut completion time 40%; production practices: resume "from where an error occurred instead of restarting", retry logic and "regular checkpoints", tell the model when a tool is failing, rainbow deployments; LLM-as-judge single call 0.0–1.0 with rubric (factual accuracy, citation accuracy, completeness, source quality, tool efficiency) "aligned best with human judgments". — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- Claude Code hooks reference: events include SessionStart, UserPromptSubmit, PreToolUse (returns `permissionDecision` allow/deny/ask/defer and `updatedInput`), PermissionRequest, PermissionDenied ("when a tool call is denied by the auto mode classifier... `retry: true` tells the model it may retry"), PostToolUse (`updatedToolOutput`; `decision: "block"` feeds reason back to Claude), PostToolUseFailure, PostToolBatch (can stop the agentic loop before the next model call), Stop/SubagentStop (`decision: "block"` keeps the agent working), PreCompact/PostCompact, PreModelSwitch, TaskCreated/Completed. Hook handlers can be shell commands, HTTP endpoints, MCP tools, LLM prompts or subagents. Exit 2 blocks (stderr is the reason); exit 1 is non-blocking. Timeouts: 600 s command/http/mcp, 30 s prompt hooks, 60 s agent hooks; a timed-out PreToolUse command hook does not block unless `"onFailure": "block"` (v2.1.295+). — [code.claude.com hooks](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks)
- Claude Code permissions: rules evaluate "deny, then ask, then allow"; "PreToolUse hooks run before the permission prompt"; "PreToolUse hook decisions don't bypass permission rules" (deny/ask still apply even if a hook says allow); a hook exiting 2 "stops the tool call before permission rules are evaluated"; a mod handling `tool.check` answers after rules and hooks, and a call it approves "runs without a classifier check". — [code.claude.com permissions](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permissions)
- Claude Code auto mode engineering post (John Hughes, 2026-03-25): two-stage classifier — "a fast single-token yes/no filter tuned to err toward blocking", then chain-of-thought review only for flagged actions; ran on Sonnet 4.6 at publication; sees user messages and tool calls "including the executable payload" but not Claude's own messages, tool outputs or tool results ("to limit rationalization and prompt injection"). Measured: users approve 93% of manual permission prompts; real internal traffic (n=10,000) false-positive rate 8.5% at stage 1, 0.4% full pipeline; real overeager actions (n=52) false-negative 6.6% stage 1, 17% full pipeline; synthetic exfiltration (n=1,000) false-negative 1.8% stage 1, 5.7% full. Tiers: built-in read-only allowlist and narrow allow rules (broad rules that permit arbitrary code execution are dropped on entering auto mode); file writes inside the project skip the classifier; everything else is classified. After 3 consecutive or 20 total denials the model stops and escalates to the human; headless (`claude -p`) terminates. Prompt injection handled by "a separate server-side probe on tool results". "It is not a drop-in replacement for careful human review on high-stakes infrastructure." — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-auto-mode)
- Claude Code permission-modes doc (current): auto mode is the default starting mode from v2.1.283; the classifier "runs on Claude Sonnet 5 by default" (server-side configured model takes precedence); decision order: (1) deny/ask/allow rules, read-only tools and connector prompts, (2) in-project writes skip the classifier, (3) "everything else goes to the classifier", (4) a block returns a rule name such as `[Data Exfiltration]`. Fail-closed: when the classifier's response doesn't parse or a separate safety check refuses its request, "Claude Code denies the action"; under server-side review it denies on no verdict and "stops the turn after ten responses in a row with no verdict". The 3-in-a-row / 20-total thresholds "are not configurable". Before destructive commands (`git reset --hard`, `rm -rf`) Claude Code runs `git status` itself and shows the classifier whether uncommitted work exists; critical-path removals (`rm -rf /`, bare `*` globs) are denied outright before the classifier sees them. Conversational boundaries ("don't push") act as block signals but "can be lost if context compaction removes the message"; "for a hard guarantee, add a deny rule". The classifier also reviews inter-agent SendMessage and subagent final reports. Each check "adds a round-trip before execution". PostToolUse hooks can attach `classifierContext` (v2.1.236+). — [code.claude.com permission-modes](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permission-modes)

### Inferences
- The interception surface has standardized: pre-tool (PreToolUse / wrap_tool_call / tool guardrail / before_tool_callback / approval policy), post-tool (PostToolUse / tool output guardrail), stop (Stop hook / PostToolBatch / output guardrail) and compaction (PreCompact / Summarization trigger). A decision engine that speaks "state + typed question → probability" can be mounted at all four in every framework surveyed without framework changes.
- Only Anthropic has published a confusion matrix for a learned gate on real traffic. Its design — a fast stage tuned to over-block, a slow stage to recover precision, deterministic tiers above and below — is the template the Jev integrations approximate with a single threshold. The 8.5% → 0.4% FP reduction from adding a second stage is the clearest measured argument for escalation-on-uncertainty rather than one-shot thresholds.
- Anthropic's own cost data (harness 20x more expensive than a solo run; evaluator "unnecessary overhead" inside the model's solo-capability boundary) is the strongest argument for making "does this step need verification at all?" itself a cheap decision.
- The Jev integrations differ on failure semantics in an instructive way: eve `auto()` fails closed to a human (authorization), LangChain AutoMode fails to an error ToolMessage (model retries), jev-skill-router fails open (advisory). The pattern is: advisory decisions fail open, authorization decisions fail closed, and Claude Code enforces fail-closed at the classifier (deny on no verdict).

- The Vercel "5–18x" figure is a quoted anecdote; no p95 latency, accuracy delta or traffic volume was published; the "~18x at p95" phrasing in the brief could not be sourced.
- The claim that "harness setup alone can shift benchmark results by 5 or more percentage points" (attributed to Anthropic's 2026 Agentic Coding Trends Report) was not found in the retrieved summaries of that report.
- Anthropic's post does not disclose stage-1 vs full-pipeline latency.

## Key question 3: Verification and self-correction — PRMs and step-level verifiers, generate-then-verify, LLM-as-judge of tool outputs, reflection, independent vs same-model verifiers, critic models

### Takeaway
Intrinsic self-correction without external signal does not work (Huang et al.; SCoRe's own framing; Anthropic's "self-evaluation bias"), but verification with an independent signal — unit tests, environment state, a separately trained verifier, or a separately prompted evaluator — reliably does, and small purpose-trained verifiers (3B–8B) now beat GPT-4o as step-level judges at roughly 10–100x lower cost; the open risk is that cheap verifiers are themselves manipulable by benign-looking context.

### Cited Findings
- Huang et al., "Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet" (ICLR 2024; arXiv 2310.01798): LLMs "struggle to self-correct their responses without external feedback, and at times, their performance even degrades after self-correction"; earlier positive results relied on oracle labels to decide when to stop correcting. — [arXiv 2310.01798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)
- Tyen et al.: poor self-correction "stems from LLMs' inability to find logical mistakes, rather than their ability to correct a known mistake" — i.e., error localization is the bottleneck. — [arXiv 2311.08516](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.08516v3)
- SCoRe (Kumar, Zhuang et al., Google DeepMind; arXiv 2409.12917; Sep 2024): self-correction "has consistently been found to be largely ineffective in modern LLMs"; SFT on correction traces fails via distribution mismatch and "behavior collapse"; multi-turn online RL yields +15.6% on MATH and +9.1% on HumanEval self-correction gains (Gemini 1.0 Pro / 1.5 Flash). — [arXiv 2409.12917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12917)
- Reflexion (Shinn et al. 2023): 91% pass@1 on HumanEval vs 80% for GPT-4, with feedback that can be "external or internally simulated" (unit tests in the coding setting). — [arXiv 2303.11366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366)
- Panickssery, Bowman, Feng (NeurIPS 2024): LLM evaluators recognize their own outputs with non-trivial accuracy and fine-tuning recognition up or down produces "a linear correlation between self-recognition capability and the strength of self-preference bias" — a causal case for independent verifiers. — [arXiv 2404.13076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076v1)
- CriticGPT (OpenAI; arXiv 2407.00215; Jul 2024): on code with naturally occurring LLM errors, "model-written critiques are preferred over human critiques in 63% of cases"; critics catch more bugs than paid human contractors; human+critic teams outperform unassisted humans 60% of the time with fewer hallucinated problems. — [arXiv 2407.00215](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2407.00215)
- Anthropic harness post (2026-03-24): "agents tended to praise their own work, even when its quality was mediocre"; QA agents "identified real issues, then talked themselves into approving the work"; separate evaluator calibrated with few-shot score breakdowns and Playwright interaction; "tuning a standalone evaluator to be skeptical was found to be far more tractable than making a generator critical of its own work". — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)
- Anthropic long-running harness (2025-11-26): unit tests and curl checks "were not enough"; end-to-end browser testing required for the model to notice features that did not work. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents)
- MAST intervention: adding a high-level verification step to ChatDev gave +15.6% absolute task success on ProgramDev; verification failures (none/incomplete 6.82%, incorrect 6.66%, premature termination 7.82%) are 21.30% of all failures. — [arXiv 2503.13657](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v2)
- Web-Shepherd (Chae et al.; arXiv 2505.15277; May 2025): first PRM for web navigation; 3B (Qwen2.5-3B) and 8B variants; "about 30 points better accuracy compared to using GPT-4o on WebRewardBench" (trajectory accuracy on WebArena test 60.0 vs 15.0); as a reranker for a GPT-4o-mini policy on WebArena-lite, success rises from 23.64% (no search) to 34.55% (8B, +10.9) or 32.12% (3B), whereas GPT-4o-mini as its own verifier gives 24.24%; cost $4.67 per 1,000 instances vs $43.57 for GPT-4o-mini and $435.74 for GPT-4o as verifier (~10x and ~100x). — [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2505.15277)
- Verifiable Process Rewards (Yuan et al.; arXiv 2605.10325; May 2026, v3 Oct 2026): repurposes symbolic/algorithmic task oracles (Sokoban search, Sudoku constraints, Minesweeper posteriors) as action-level verifiers; on τ²-bench uses an expert LLM as reference policy; outperforms outcome-only GRPO; depends on "the availability and reliability of task oracles". — [arXiv 2605.10325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10325)
- Best-of-N with verifiers on SWE-bench Verified (500 tasks): Agentic Rubrics reranking 16 rollouts — Best@16 40.6% (Qwen3-32B) and 54.2% (Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B) vs self-consistency 33.2%/47.6%, a patch classifier 37.1%/50.2%, and oracle ceilings 51.4%/65.6%. SWE-World verifier ranking lifts SWR-32B from 55.0% (K=1) to 68.2% (TTS@8) vs 59.4% (R2E-Gym-Verifier-14B) and 59.6% (OpenHands-32B-Verifier) at the same budget. Satori-SWE: reward model + unit tests over 50 candidates gives Best@50 41.6% (32B), matching Llama3-SWE-RL-70B's Best@500. — [arXiv 2601.04171](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04171); [arXiv 2602.03419](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.03419); [arXiv 2505.23604](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.23604)
- Anthropic eval guide: model-based graders need calibration against humans; give the judge an escape option ("Unknown"); "grade each dimension with an isolated LLM judge rather than one judge for all dimensions"; "You won't know if your graders are working well unless you read the transcripts". — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)
- Trajectory-based early outcome prediction (Cao; arXiv 2609.25647; 2026-09-22): predicting success/failure from partial SWE-bench Verified trajectories to stop runs early; calibration does not transfer uniformly across agents — persistent corrected calibration gaps for gpt-5-mini/SUCCESS (0.1377) and claude-opus-4.6/FAILURE (0.1107) — so a predictor must be recalibrated per target agent. — [arXiv 2609.25647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25647)
- JevOut (Xu et al.; arXiv 2609.30243; v1 2026-09-24): across four decision systems including Jev and seven datasets (knowledge, reasoning, tool routing), short "natural" context added to inputs — background or procedural text, not instructions — redirected 61.4% to 73.2% of initially correct decisions to a pre-chosen wrong option within 64 evaluations per decision, often "high-confidence wrong answers"; contexts transfer across models; 91.6% of 250 sampled contexts judged natural and answer-preserving by human majority. — [arXiv 2609.30243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30243)
- Pydantic AI documented Jev weaknesses: literal reading ("answers the question you wrote, not the one you meant"), arithmetic/counting/dates, indirection and irrelevant context, "injected instructions can move answers", a question and its negation "need not sum to one", and asked whether it can answer "it hands off almost everything". — [Pydantic AI docs](https://pydantic.dev/docs/ai/models/typesafe/)
- TypeSafe/InfoQ: Jev trained with "Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions"; "the article gives no calibration metrics"; jev-1.13 jaggedness docs list unreliable counting, arithmetic, date comparison and accuracy loss on large noisy state. — [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/typesafe-ai-jev-released/)

### Inferences
- The evidence sorts verification by signal source: (1) environment ground truth (tests, browser, DB state) — strongest; (2) independently trained verifier/PRM — strong and now cheap; (3) separately prompted evaluator of the same model family — useful but lenient, needs calibration; (4) same-model self-critique — unreliable. A cheap calibrated judge belongs in tier 2/3 and should be fed tier-1 signals (tool results, test output) rather than asked to reason from scratch.
- Web-Shepherd and the SWE-bench verifier papers show a verifier at ~1/10 the policy's cost can add 8–13 absolute points via reranking; the oracle ceilings (51–66%) show how much headroom remains for better verifiers — verifier accuracy, not sampling budget, is the binding constraint.
- JevOut is the main caution for the compounding-decision-engine thesis: a verifier that reads untrusted content (web pages, tool outputs) can be steered by non-instructional text. Claude Code's design (strip tool results from the gate; separate injection probe) is the current mitigation; a Jev gate should likewise be fed structured, trusted state rather than raw tool output where possible.

### Gaps
- No independent measurement of Jev's calibration (reliability diagrams, ECE) was found; TypeSafe has published none.
- VPR's reported finding that "weak verifiers can degrade in-domain and OOD performance" appeared in a search snippet but not in the retrieved abstract; numbers unconfirmed.
- No study measuring an LLM-as-judge specifically on tool outputs (as opposed to final answers or trajectories) with precision/recall was found.

## Key question 4: Sampling-based reliability — best-of-N with cheap judges, voting, speculative fan-out, tree search over actions, and their costs

### Takeaway
Parallel sampling plus a verifier is the best-evidenced way to buy reliability at a known cost (verifier reranking adds ~8–13 points on SWE-bench Verified; parallel beats serial compute on GSO), tree search over actions (LATS) gives large gains but at explicitly "higher computational cost", and the Jev-style speculative fan-out of candidate decisions has so far been measured only as a latency/efficiency win (25% faster, 10x fewer browser calls), not an accuracy win.

### Cited Findings
- LATS (Zhou et al.; arXiv 2310.04406; ICML 2024): HumanEval pass@1 92.7 with GPT-4 (base 80.1; Reflexion 91.0), 83.8 with GPT-3.5 (ReAct 56.9); HotPotQA EM 0.63 vs ReAct 0.32; WebShop score 75.9 / success 38.0 vs ReAct best-of-k 59.1 / 32.0; token cost on HotPotQA successful runs 173,290 (LATS) vs 210,215 (ToT) vs 176,500 (RAP); 66.65 nodes expanded at k=50; complexity O(kn); removing the LM value heuristic drops EM 0.63 → 0.37, removing reflection 0.63 → 0.58, replacing MCTS with DFS 0.63 → 0.42; authors acknowledge "a higher computational cost" than ReAct or Reflexion. — [arXiv 2310.04406](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.04406)
- GSO (software optimization benchmark): "parallel compute scales more efficiently than serial compute"; for o4-mini, 8 rollouts of 50 steps beat a single 400-step rollout. — [arXiv 2505.23671](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.23671)
- Verifier-based best-of-N on SWE-bench Verified: see KQ3 (Agentic Rubrics Best@16 40.6/54.2 vs self-consistency 33.2/47.6; SWE-World 55.0 → 68.2 at TTS@8; Satori-SWE Best@50 41.6 matching a 70B model's Best@500). Self-consistency (majority voting) was the weakest selector in Agentic Rubrics. — [arXiv 2601.04171](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04171); [arXiv 2602.03419](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.03419); [arXiv 2505.23604](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.23604)
- Web-Shepherd as a cheap judge for trajectory search: +10.9 points on WebArena-lite at ~1/10 the cost of GPT-4o-mini as judge. — [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2505.15277)
- Anthropic "Building effective agents": parallelization via sectioning and "voting" ("running the same task multiple times... when multiple perspectives raise confidence"). — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)
- Anthropic multi-agent research: parallelization cut research time "up to 90%"; early agents "spawned 50 subagents for simple queries" until effort-scaling rules were added (1 agent / 3–10 tool calls for simple fact-finding; 2–4 subagents with 10–15 calls for comparisons; >10 subagents for complex research). — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- browser-use/jev-ultrafast (MIT, Browser Use org; self-reported README): each decision cycle sends one TypeSafe request asking the operation and the targets in parallel (`click_target`, `type_text_target`, `select_target`); "only the target matching the chosen operation executes"; a small LLM (`inception/mercury-2.5`, reasoning off) generates text only for TYPE_TEXT. Measured: Zürich → London Google Flights search 7,073 ms; over six alternating runs of one task both versions passed 3/3, median task time 9.450 s → 7.092 s (25% faster), median browser-protocol calls 1,092 → 101; Wikipedia article 2.798 s; hotel search/filter 1.896 s; README states 3/3 "is not a general reliability benchmark". — [jev-ultrafast README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/browser-use/jev-ultrafast/main/README.md); secondary cost claim ~US$0.0039 per flight search — [Inside (Taiwan)](https://www.inside.com.tw/article/42431-jev-ultrafast-browser-agent-gregpr07)
- TypeSafe "speculative fan-out" pattern docs: "Send many questions in one API call, including ones that may turn out to be irrelevant, and let your code decide which answers to use"; "adding more questions usually has little effect on response time"; worked example asks 5 questions (1 choice, 2 score, 2 noul) with thresholds `bug_severity.score > 1.5`, `bug_repro.noul > 0.6`, `refund.noul > 0.7`, `frustration.score > 1.5`. — [TypeSafe docs](https://docs.typesafe.ai/patterns/fan-out)
- Jev latency and cost (vendor and crowd): TypeSafe quotes 70–500 ms end to end, $0.042/M input tokens, output free; OpenChamber's analysis of 12,759 launch tweets found a median user-reported speedup of 7x (vs the 193.6x headline), median cost saving 30x, median latency 76 ms with upper quartile 270 ms. — [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/typesafe-ai-jev-released/)
- MarkTechPost's vendor-eval roundup lists Jev at 67.8% mean accuracy, $0.0004 per case, 0.4 s on TypeSafe's own workflow evals (pages returned 403 on fetch; figures from search snippets). — [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/02/decision-ai-models-explained-typesafe-jev-vs-fastino-glide-gliner2-5-decide-and-open-source-competitors/)

### Inferences
- The cost structure differs sharply: tree search multiplies policy-model tokens (LATS ~66 node expansions at k=50); best-of-N multiplies policy tokens by N but needs only one cheap verifier pass per candidate; speculative fan-out of decisions multiplies only decision-model questions, which TypeSafe prices near zero. This makes fan-out the cheapest place to add redundancy, but the measured benefit so far is latency, not accuracy.
- Majority voting is consistently the weakest selector in the agent literature (Agentic Rubrics); a calibrated judge over candidate trajectories or candidate actions is where the gains are.
- jev-ultrafast's 10x reduction in browser-protocol calls suggests the main engineering payoff of a decision model in the loop is collapsing multi-call "observe → think → act" cycles into one round trip, which also shortens the window for stale-state errors.

### Gaps
- No accuracy or pass^k measurement of jev-ultrafast or any speculative-fan-out agent beyond 3/3 on one task.
- No Browserbase/Stagehand integration with Jev was found; the "Jev Browser" MCP listing is a separate project.
- No published token/cost figures for Tree of Thoughts on agent tasks; LATS reports tokens only for HotPotQA.

## Key question 5: Checkpointing and reversibility — durable execution, git-based rollback, sandboxing, two-way-door classification, undo logs, and whether reversibility lets systems tolerate lower per-step accuracy

### Takeaway
The measured case for reversibility is indirect but strong: mutating actions account for nearly all compounding failure (SABER), environment-enforced confirmation on irreversible calls cut harm from 37.5–60% to 2.5% (GateArena), and prompt-only "ask before irreversible action" instructions were skipped by two of three model backbones in 38–48% of episodes — so harnesses now classify actions by reversibility (Claude Code's in-project writes skip the gate because they are checkpointed) and bind approvals to specific calls rather than trusting the model to pause.

### Cited Findings
- SABER: deviations in mutating actions reduce odds of success by up to 92% (Airline) / 96% (Retail); non-mutating deviations have little effect; mutation-gated verification plus targeted reflection before mutating steps and block-based context cleaning gave Qwen3-Thinking +28% relative on Airline, +11% Retail, +7% SWE-bench Verified; Claude +9% / +7%. — [arXiv 2512.07850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07850)
- GateArena (Jiang, Quinzan, Bhatt; NeurIPS 2026): with the only safeguard a system-prompt instruction, three backbones attempted to call a withheld confirmation tool in 38%, 48% and <1% of episodes with no adversarial input; fix is a single-use confirmation token "locked to specific parameter values" that the execution tool checks; across 1,880 episodes at a 60% request-deviation rate, ungated execution caused 37.5–60% irreversible harm vs 2.5% gated. — [NeurIPS 2026](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/168290)
- "Same Action, Different Justification" (ICML 2026): matched pairs with identical irreversible actions differing only in justification path; keyword guardrails separate none; endpoint-only judges "remain near zero on delegated scope and chain verifiability"; path-based authorization (CCA) reaches full separation on five pair families. — [ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/77995)
- Claude Code checkpointing: automatic checkpoint per user prompt, `/rewind` (or Esc Esc) restores code and/or conversation; tracks only Claude's own edits via Write/Edit/NotebookEdit (not manual or Bash edits); kept ~30 days; independent of git. — [code.claude.com checkpointing](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/checkpointing)
- Claude Code auto mode tiers: "File writes inside the project directory skip the classifier"; everything else classified; before `git reset --hard` / `rm -rf`, Claude Code runs `git status` and shows the classifier whether uncommitted work exists; critical-path removals denied deterministically. — [Anthropic auto mode](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-auto-mode); [permission-modes](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permission-modes)
- Anthropic long-running harness: descriptive git commits "let agents revert bad changes and recover working states"; feature_list.json in JSON so the model is "less likely to inappropriately overwrite it". — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents)
- Anthropic multi-agent system: agents "resume from where an error occurred instead of restarting"; "regular checkpoints" plus retry logic as deterministic safeguards. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- Temporal durable execution for agents: model calls as Activities with durable retries, skipped on replay; "Therefore, only the latest LangGraph checkpoint is retained, with the understanding that Temporal's Workflow replay can restore an agent to any previous state"; caveat that replay does not undo external effects — "An agent that crashed after issuing a refund, but before recording that it did, may issue the refund again on restart." — [Temporal docs](https://docs.temporal.io/develop/python/integrations/openai-agents); [Temporal blog (Manetu)](https://temporal.io/blog/manetu-the-thread-is-the-workflow)
- LangGraph time travel: resume from a prior checkpoint and fork history; HumanInTheLoopMiddleware requires a checkpointer. — [LangChain built-in middleware](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langchain/middleware/built-in)
- Cloudflare `waitForApproval()` and eve `auto()`/`always()` park the run durably ("seconds or days"; "months or longer") with nothing held in memory. — [Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/agents/guides/human-in-the-loop); [eve](https://eve.dev/docs/human-in-the-loop)
- AgentAbstain (Jul 2026): paired-task benchmark of 8 abstention scenarios "across pre-execution reasoning and runtime discovery", motivated by "unintended irreversible actions". CDWM (NeurIPS 2026) "explicitly factor[s] out reversibility, irreversible cost and prediction confidence" and reports fewer irreversible failures on web and game benchmarks. — [AgentAbstain](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.10059); [CDWM](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/154820)
- OpenAI agent guide: human intervention for "high-risk, sensitive, or irreversible actions". — [ibl.ai summary](https://ibl.ai/blog/openai-a-practical-guide-to-building-agents.md)
- Illusion paper's horizon formula assumes "no self-correction"; best-of-N results (KQ3/KQ4) are only possible because sandboxed rollouts are discardable. — [arXiv 2509.09677](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09677)

### Inferences
- Reversibility is the variable that converts a per-step accuracy requirement into a per-irreversible-step requirement: if mutating steps are a minority and every mutating step is gated or checkpointed, the effective horizon is governed by the accuracy on gated steps plus the gate's recall, not by raw p. SABER's 92–96% odds effect on mutating actions versus "little or no effect" for non-mutating ones is the closest thing to a direct measurement.
- Claude Code's tiering is an implicit two-way-door classifier: reversible in-project writes (checkpointed) are ungated; irreversible or externally visible actions are gated; catastrophic ones are hard-denied. A decision engine's first job at a tool boundary is therefore reversibility classification, after which the required confidence threshold differs by class (Vercel's 0.7 vs 0.9 guidance).
- GateArena's 38–48% confirmation-skip rate means "ask the model to ask" is not a gate; the gate must be enforced by the environment (hook, token, approval policy), which is exactly where a sub-second judgment slots in without waiting on a human for every call.

### Gaps
- No study was found that directly measures "tolerable per-step accuracy as a function of rollback coverage" in LLM agents; the relationship is inferred from SABER, GateArena and the horizon formula.
- No published reversibility taxonomy of tool calls with measured classifier accuracy; the ICML/NeurIPS 2026 papers test gating designs, not reversibility labelling.
- No measurements on how often Claude Code `/rewind` is used after a bad step or how much it recovers.

## Key question 6: Context management as a decision problem — compaction triggers, tool-output screening, relevance scoring (fast-jev-compaction), retrieval chunk filtering

### Takeaway
Context is now treated as an "attention budget" where keeping the wrong tokens (especially the model's own prior errors) measurably degrades later steps, but blunt truncation makes decay worse; the Jev-based compaction plugin shows a per-item keep/truncate/remove decision can run in about a second over ~1M tokens, yet its quality relative to summarization is unmeasured.

### Cited Findings
- Anthropic context engineering (2025-09-29): "context rot" — recall declines as tokens grow, "a performance gradient rather than a hard cliff"; compaction should keep architectural decisions, unresolved bugs and implementation details and drop redundant tool outputs; "maximize recall first... then improve precision"; "tool result clearing" is "one of the safest, lightest-touch forms of compaction"; Claude Code continues after compaction with the summary plus "the five most recently accessed files"; sub-agents explore with tens of thousands of tokens and return 1,000–2,000-token summaries. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)
- Illusion paper: self-conditioning on prior errors raises error rates; CoT does not fix it; thinking models (e.g., Qwen3 thinking) "do not self-condition"; CoT histories "exhaust context windows quickly". — [arXiv 2509.09677](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09677)
- Mittal: bounding the context window made per-step decay steeper (logit slope −0.69 vs −0.44) — "cuts against... limiting context as a production shortcut". — [arXiv 2609.01660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01660)
- MAST: loss of conversation history is 3.33% of failures; conversation reset 2.33%. — [arXiv 2503.13657](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v2)
- Claude Code: conversational boundaries read by the auto-mode classifier "can be lost if context compaction removes the message that stated it" — compaction decisions have safety consequences; PreCompact hooks can block compaction. — [permission-modes](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permission-modes); [hooks](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks)
- fast-jev-compaction (Tamara Tran, MIT; Claude Code plugin; covered 2026-09-23): "scores every tool call/result pair with Jev in a single request", decides keep / truncate / remove per pair, keeps kept content verbatim, compresses further by truncating inputs and collapsing old messages if needed, and "falls back to the built-in summarization" if not enough space is freed; Alex Volkov reported one session going "from nearly 1M tokens to 86K in about one second"; repo passed 6k stars within days. Reviewer caveats (archived public post): Jev sees tool calls but not their outputs; its 32K context forces chunking; unclear whether quality beats default compaction. — [dsebastien.net](https://www.dsebastien.net/fast-jev-compaction/); [repo](https://github.com/tamaratran/fast-jev-compaction); [hermes-ai archive](https://hermes-ai.net/uk/jev/case/2100913118528393596/)
- LangChain: SummarizationMiddleware triggers on token/message/fraction thresholds (summary written by an LLM); ContextEditingMiddleware deterministically clears tool outputs above `trigger` tokens, keeping the latest `keep`. — [LangChain docs](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langchain/middleware/built-in)
- Pydantic AI: Jev conversations "need compaction" and "summaries need a language model because Jev does not write text"; accuracy "drops with multi-hop questions or extra state". — [Pydantic AI docs](https://pydantic.dev/docs/ai/models/typesafe/)
- SABER's "block-based context cleaning" is one of its three components and part of the measured +7% to +28% gains. — [arXiv 2512.07850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07850)
- Anthropic auto mode: stripping tool results from the classifier's input is itself a context-screening decision ("so hostile content in a file or web page can't manipulate the classifier directly"); PostToolUse hooks can inject `classifierContext`. — [permission-modes](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permission-modes)

### Inferences
- The two empirical results point to a specific compaction policy: remove the agent's own failed attempts and stale tool output (self-conditioning), but do not shrink context indiscriminately (Mittal). That is a per-item relevance-and-harm judgment — the shape of question Jev answers — rather than a token-count trigger.
- A compaction decision model should see the tool result, not just the call: the fast-jev-compaction critique (decisions made on calls alone) and Anthropic's "tool result clearing" guidance both locate the value in the outputs.
- Compaction interacts with safety: a decision engine that drops a user-stated boundary silently weakens later gates; "pin" decisions (never compact) are as important as "drop" decisions.

### Gaps
- No benchmark comparing Jev-scored compaction against summary-based compaction on downstream task success; the 1M → 86K figure is a single user anecdote.
- No sources found on yes/no judge filtering of retrieval chunks with measured precision/recall in agent settings within this research pass.
- Anthropic's context-engineering post gives no token-reduction percentages or benchmark deltas.

## Key question 7: Routing — model routing by difficulty, skill and tool selection, measured cost/quality trade-offs

### Takeaway
Routing is the best-measured use of cheap judgments: RouteLLM holds 95% of GPT-4 quality while sending 14–26% of queries to the strong model, ReDAct matches large-model agent quality while deferring ~15% of decisions, and the Jev skill-router cut wrong-skill loads roughly in half on a 488-request set (self-reported) at ~$0.00002 and ~1 s per turn.

### Cited Findings
- RouteLLM (LMSYS, 2024-07-01): routers trained on Chatbot Arena preferences; "one router achieves 95% of GPT-4 performance using 26% GPT-4 calls", and with LLM-judge data augmentation matrix factorization needs "only 14% of total calls"; cost savings relative to a random baseline >85% on MT Bench, 45% on MMLU, 35% on GSM8K at 95% quality. — [LMSYS blog](https://lmsys.org/blog/2024-07-01-routellm/)
- Not Diamond: third-party RouterArena score 57.29 (from a competitor's write-up); vendor-attributed ~5% accuracy gains, ~30% cost savings, 100–200 ms routing overhead, $0.05/M tokens routed — all unverified secondary figures. — [Lynkr dev.to post](https://dev.to/lynkr/we-put-our-router-on-an-academic-benchmark-here-are-the-numbers-wed-rather-hide-1c83); [tool directory](https://tooldirectory.ai/tools/not-diamond)
- ReDAct (Piatrashyn et al.; arXiv 2604.07036; 2026-04-08): small model acts by default; when its predictive uncertainty exceeds a calibrated threshold the decision defers to a large model; on ALFWorld and MiniGrid "deferring only about 15% of decisions to the large model" matches large-model-only quality at much lower cost; the threshold is chosen so the calibration-set call rate hits a target. — [arXiv 2604.07036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07036)
- LangChain ModelRouterMiddleware (Jev): routes once per run on the latest human message; the chosen model handles every call in the run; probabilities/confidence kept in state. — [LangChain TypeSafe docs](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/integrations/providers/typesafe.md)
- Pydantic AI: Jev's route question covers every tool plus output type; "Jev always takes the likeliest route"; low-confidence answers escalate via `FallbackModel`; "Tools needing arguments the text does not state are proposed to a language model behind Jev". — [Pydantic AI docs](https://pydantic.dev/docs/ai/models/typesafe/)
- jev-skill-router (Hermes Agent community plugin by DoGMaTiiC; v0.1.0 listed 2026-09-23): two Jev calls per eligible turn — (1) one Choice over the whole skill roster (chunked at 240 per question with "none of these") plus three gate booleans (one inverted), silent if gate mean < 0.30; (2) re-read top-3 candidates with up to 700 chars of SKILL.md each, silent if winner's fit < 0.40; otherwise injects one `<skill_relevance>` line. Author's 76-request calibration set (66 one-skill, 10 no-skill): 95% top-1, 0/10 needless suggestions, second stage right 66/66 ("every loss came from the gate"). Live test 2026-09-21: TypeSafe direct 1,431 ms vs gateway 732 ms; ~$0.00002 per eligible turn on the gateway, ~1 s latency, worst-case ~10 s. Fails open on missing key, timeout, HTTP error or malformed response; retries once on 429/529 only if Retry-After ≤2 s; goes silent 120 s after 3 consecutive rate limits. Ground-truth labelling not described. — [Hermes docs](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/plugins/jev-skill-router)
- The 488-request figures: "Measured on the Hermes catalog (TypeSafe's cookbook, 488 requests, jev-1.12): wrong skill loads 16.8% → 7.3%, needless loads 9.8% → 4.0%"; motivation — Hermes truncates skill index descriptions to ~60 characters so "at 190+ skills the agent is left guessing". This text appeared in a localized docs variant via search; the English page fetched shows the 76-request calibration instead. Self-reported, no independent replication. — [Hermes docs (zh-Hans)](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/zh-Hans/plugins/jev-skill-router)
- LangChain LLMToolSelectorMiddleware "can use a cheaper model" to pick tools before the main call. — [LangChain docs](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/langchain/middleware/built-in)
- Anthropic: rewriting tool descriptions cut task completion time 40%; "If a human cannot easily pick the right tool, the agent likely cannot either." — [Anthropic multi-agent](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system); [context engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)
- Vercel KB routing thresholds: confidence ≥0.6 and probability ≥0.7 to auto-assign, else human. — [Vercel KB](https://vercel.com/kb/guide/typesafe-jev-and-ai-sdk)
- Bryo AI (via InfoQ): Gemini "slightly more accurate" than Jev on email classification but "10 to 20 times more expensive"; valued Jev as "the only option that returns a real probability". — [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/typesafe-ai-jev-released/)
- Armin Ronacher (via TechCrunch/InfoQ): Jev "delegates the hallucination problem a little bit to the user" but "saw model routing as a good fit". — [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/typesafe-ai-jev-released/)

### Inferences
- Routing results consistently show 70–85% of calls can go to the cheap path at ~95% quality when the router is calibrated; the agent-specific result (ReDAct) matters because it routes per decision, not per conversation, which is where the LangChain middleware (once per run) is weakest.
- The skill-router's finding that "every loss came from the gate" (should-we-act-at-all) rather than from choosing among candidates is a recurring shape: the hard decision is abstention, not selection. That argues for spending calibration effort on the gate threshold.
- Two-stage routing (cheap over all candidates, then re-read top-k with richer descriptions) is the same escalation shape as Claude Code's two-stage classifier; both pay latency only on the uncertain tail.

### Gaps
- No independent measurement of any Jev router; Not Diamond's own numbers were not retrievable.
- RouteLLM's figures are from 2024 chat benchmarks, not agent tasks; no 2025–2026 agent-task routing benchmark with cost/quality curves was found besides ReDAct.
- No published evaluation of ModelRouterMiddleware's routing accuracy or of per-turn re-routing.

## Key question 8: Safety gates — tool-call risk classification, prompt-injection screening, combining hard rules with learned judgments

### Takeaway
Production gates layer deterministic rules around a learned classifier: hard denies and allowlists first, a fast learned judgment for the middle, escalation (second stage, human, or deny-on-no-verdict) for the uncertain tail — Claude Code's version achieves 0.4% false positives but still misses 17% of real overeager actions, and the measured cost of continuous monitoring drops two orders of magnitude when the judge is a decision model ($2.94 vs $372 in one demo).

### Cited Findings
- Claude Code auto mode confusion matrix (n=10,000 real actions: FP 8.5% → 0.4% with second stage; n=52 real overeager: FN 6.6% → 17%; n=1,000 synthetic exfiltration: FN 1.8% → 5.7%); stage 1 "tuned to err toward blocking"; blocks grouped as destroying/exfiltrating data, degrading security posture, crossing trust boundaries, bypassing review/affecting others; separate injection probe on tool results; multi-agent handoff checks at delegation and return. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-auto-mode)
- Claude Code hard floors: deny rules precede everything ("An allow rule can't carve an exception out of a deny rule"); exit-2 PreToolUse hooks block before rules; critical-path `rm -rf` and bare-glob `Remove-Item` denied "before the classifier sees it"; deny on unparseable or refused classifier response; conversational boundaries are soft ("add a deny rule" for a hard guarantee); broad code-execution allow rules dropped on entering auto mode. — [permission-modes](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permission-modes); [permissions](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permissions)
- Vercel per-class thresholds: ~0.7 for read-only, ≥0.9 for destructive with confirmation below; separate boolean questions for "destructive intent" and "production impact" plus a choice for command category; "Jev classifies"; authorization stays in code. — [Vercel KB](https://vercel.com/kb/guide/typesafe-jev-and-ai-sdk)
- Vercel/TechCrunch: Jev replaced an OpenAI model in a command-safety classifier, "five to 18 times more quickly and with greater accuracy" (self-reported). — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/18/a-new-kind-of-ai-model-from-a-chatgpt-inventor-is-thrilling-developers/)
- eve `auto()`: clear/caution via Jev on tool name + input; caution, failed review or incomplete input all require human approval (fails closed). — [eve docs](https://eve.dev/docs/human-in-the-loop)
- LangChain AutoModeMiddleware: blocks by returning an error ToolMessage; only listed tools; privacy warning that TypeSafe receives tool arguments and conversation state. — [LangChain TypeSafe docs](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/integrations/providers/typesafe.md)
- OpenAI Agents SDK: parallel vs blocking guardrails trade latency for wasted tokens; tool guardrails can skip/replace/raise; example rejects tool inputs containing `sk-`. — [OpenAI docs](https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-python/guardrails/)
- OpenAI monitors agents with "a separate model to watch for bad agent actions, at 'significant compute cost'"; a Jev-based per-action monitor demo cost $2.94 vs $372 with a frontier LLM; blocks "actions it is highly confident are bad, flags others for review, and permits the rest". — [TechCrunch 2026-09-30](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/30/openais-jev-clone-could-help-the-frontier-lab-stop-its-swarming-agents/)
- GateArena: prompt-only confirmation skipped in 38%/48% of episodes by two backbones; environment-enforced single-use tokens cut harm to 2.5%. — [NeurIPS 2026](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/168290)
- Path-based authorization: keyword guardrails separate none of the matched irreversible-action pairs; endpoint-only judges near zero on delegated scope; path-based CCA reaches full separation. — [ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/77995)
- JevOut: natural-looking context flips 61.4–73.2% of correct decisions in four decision systems including Jev, with high confidence — prompt-injection risk for decision-model gates that read untrusted text. — [arXiv 2609.30243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30243)
- Pydantic AI: "Injected instructions can move answers"; pair Jev guards with deterministic checks. — [Pydantic AI docs](https://pydantic.dev/docs/ai/models/typesafe/)
- MAST LLM-annotator precision for failure detection (o1, few-shot): precision 0.833, recall 0.77 — an estimate of how good a frontier LLM judge is at spotting agent failures in traces. — [arXiv 2503.13657](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v2)
- TechCrunch: TypeSafe CEO "sees users deploying Jev to track LLM agent traces and prevent jailbreaks". — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/18/a-new-kind-of-ai-model-from-a-chatgpt-inventor-is-thrilling-developers/)

### Inferences
- The consistent architecture is fail-closed floors (deny lists, critical paths, hard approvals) + fail-open-to-model or fail-closed-to-human learned judgments in the middle; which failure mode the learned layer uses is determined by whether it authorizes (closed) or advises (open).
- Anthropic's 17% miss rate on real overeager actions at 0.4% FP is the current public operating point for a Sonnet-class gate; a Jev-class gate's operating point is unpublished. The 10,000-action FP measurement is the template a Jev gate needs before it can be trusted to replace a human prompt.
- JevOut plus Claude Code's input-stripping imply a design rule for cheap gates: feed them structured, trusted state (tool name, arguments, user intent, repo metadata) and keep raw web/tool content out of the state, or route any decision that depends on untrusted content to a slower stage.

### Gaps
- No published FP/FN or latency distribution for any Jev-based gate (Vercel, LangChain, eve).
- No measurement of how Claude Code's classifier latency compares across Sonnet 4.6 / Sonnet 5 / server-side review.
- No study quantifying prompt-injection susceptibility of Jev specifically as a tool-call gate (JevOut covers knowledge/reasoning/tool-routing datasets).

## Key question 9: Calibration in practice — do harnesses log judgment confidence and outcomes to re-fit thresholds, and what tooling exists

### Takeaway
Closed-loop threshold re-fitting from production outcomes is almost absent: vendors say "calibrate against labeled examples", Anthropic re-measured its gate on 10,000 real actions and collects `/feedback` on false positives, LangSmith's Align Evals calibrates judges against human golden sets, and DSPy's ReAnchor (Sep 2026) is the first tool that fits Boolean/Score/Choice thresholds against an end-to-end program metric with held-out validation — none re-fit automatically from logged outcomes.

### Cited Findings
- DSPy 3.4.0 (released 25 Sep 2026): `dspy.experimental.ReAnchor` "calibrates decisions against your program's metric", fitting "Boolean thresholds, Score cuts, and Choice weights in Predict programs without rewriting instructions, criteria, or demonstrations"; a candidate must beat the training score and pass a held-out fold check; validation set only for reporting; works with TypeSafe (`dspy.experimental.TypeSafe("jev-latest")`) and generative LMs (requests probability evidence for `bool`/`Literal`); decision rules are applied locally so cached evidence is reused; warning: "Noul confidence measures distance from its configured threshold, not statistical calibration; generative-LM confidence is self-reported". — [DSPy 3.4.0 release](https://github.com/stanfordnlp/dspy/releases/tag/3.4.0)
- Vercel KB: "Thresholds are starting points. Calibrate against labeled examples, since calibration doesn't guarantee any individual answer is correct"; confidence exposed per question in `providerMetadata`. — [Vercel KB](https://vercel.com/kb/guide/typesafe-jev-and-ai-sdk)
- Pydantic AI: "measure accuracy and hand-off rates on your own labelled data before relying on them"; pin a version "once thresholds are tuned, since `jev-latest` moves". — [Pydantic AI docs](https://pydantic.dev/docs/ai/models/typesafe/)
- Anthropic auto mode: measured FP/FN on real traffic and tuned stage 1 to over-block; `/feedback` to report false positives; thresholds (3 consecutive / 20 total denials) fixed. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-auto-mode); [permission-modes](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permission-modes)
- jev-skill-router: 76 labelled requests used to set gate 0.30 / fits 0.40; calibration covers TypeSafe direct only, not gateway routes. — [Hermes docs](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/plugins/jev-skill-router)
- ReDAct: picks the uncertainty threshold whose call rate on a calibration set is closest to a target deferral rate. — [arXiv 2604.07036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07036)
- Early-outcome prediction audit: calibration gaps persist per target agent/outcome (0.1377, 0.1107) within one benchmark — thresholds learned on one agent do not transfer. — [arXiv 2609.25647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25647)
- LangSmith Align Evals (2025-07-29): "alignment score" between LLM-judge scores and a human-graded golden set; iterate judge prompts against it; "Alignment is measured against a human-graded golden set, not production outcomes"; roadmap: evaluator analytics over time and automatic prompt optimization. LangSmith online evals run LLM-as-judge and rule-based evaluators on live traffic with automation rules routing traces to annotation queues. — [LangChain blog](https://www.langchain.com/blog/introducing-align-evals); [LangChain vs Braintrust page (vendor)](https://www.langchain.com/resources/langsmith-vs-braintrust)
- Langfuse: LLM-as-judge scores attached to production traces; deterministic online checks were "on the roadmap" at the time of a vendor comparison. Braintrust: code-first scorers. No native confidence→outcome threshold refit found for any of the three. — [LangChain vs Langfuse (vendor)](https://www.langchain.com/articles/langsmith-vs-langfuse); [langwatch comparison](https://langwatch.ai/blog/langwatch-vs-langsmith-vs-braintrust-vs-langfuse-choosing-the-best-llm-evaluation-monitoring-tool-in-2025)
- langchain-typesafe records traces and token usage in LangSmith; ModelRouterMiddleware stores `model_route` (with the Choice primitive's probabilities/confidence) in agent state — the raw material for outcome joins, but no join is built. — [LangChain TypeSafe docs](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/integrations/providers/typesafe.md); [LangChain blog](https://www.langchain.com/blog/building-a-harness-with-jev)
- Anthropic eval guide: calibrate model graders against human experts; "occasional human review is sufficient" once robust. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)
- TechCrunch: "calibration of these models' outputs to real-world conditions is an open question". InfoQ: TypeSafe publishes no calibration metrics. — [TechCrunch 2026-09-30](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/30/openais-jev-clone-could-help-the-frontier-lab-stop-its-swarming-agents/); [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/typesafe-ai-jev-released/)

### Inferences
- The pieces exist separately — per-question probabilities in state (LangChain/Vercel/Pydantic), outcome logging (LangSmith/Langfuse), threshold fitting against a metric (ReAnchor) — but no harness joins judgment confidence to downstream outcome and re-fits thresholds. This is an open seam a compounding-decision engine could own.
- Two warnings shape how to do it: ReAnchor's note that Noul "confidence" is distance-from-threshold, not calibration, and the early-outcome-prediction result that calibration is target-specific — thresholds must be fit per agent/tool/action class, not globally.

### Gaps
- No harness or observability vendor found that logs gate confidence alongside eventual task outcome and re-fits thresholds; no reliability diagrams for Jev or for Claude Code's classifier published.
- Braintrust's current online-scoring and calibration features were only available via competitor comparisons.

## Ranked decision points inside an agent loop where a sub-second calibrated judgment pays off (synthesis)

### Takeaway
Ranked by measured leverage (effect size × frequency × cost of being wrong), the top decision points are: gating mutating/irreversible tool calls, selecting among candidate trajectories or patches with an independent verifier, deciding continue/stop/escalate, choosing what to keep in context, and routing model effort — with speculative fan-out of next actions, skill/tool selection, and tool-output screening following.

### Cited Findings
- Mutating-action gating: SABER odds effects (92–96%), GateArena harm 37.5–60% → 2.5%, Claude Code gate FP 0.4% / FN 17%, per-action monitoring cost $2.94 vs $372, Vercel 5–18x anecdote. — [SABER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07850); [GateArena](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/168290); [Anthropic auto mode](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-auto-mode); [TechCrunch 09-30](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/30/openais-jev-clone-could-help-the-frontier-lab-stop-its-swarming-agents/); [TechCrunch 09-18](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/18/a-new-kind-of-ai-model-from-a-chatgpt-inventor-is-thrilling-developers/)
- Candidate selection with an independent verifier: SWE-World 55.0 → 68.2 (TTS@8); Agentic Rubrics Best@16 +7.4/+6.6 over self-consistency; Web-Shepherd +10.9 at ~1/10 cost; evaluator agent "a strong lever" vs self-evaluation bias. — [arXiv 2602.03419](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.03419); [arXiv 2601.04171](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04171); [HF 2505.15277](https://huggingface.co/papers/2505.15277); [Anthropic harness](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)
- Continue / stop / escalate: MAST premature termination 7.82% + unaware of termination 9.82% + step repetition 17.14%; Claude Code escalates after 3/20 denials; ReDAct ~15% deferral matches large-model quality; early-outcome prediction needs per-agent calibration; evaluator "unnecessary overhead" inside solo-capability boundary. — [MAST](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v2); [permission-modes](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permission-modes); [ReDAct](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07036); [arXiv 2609.25647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25647); [Anthropic harness](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)
- Context keep/drop per item: self-conditioning on prior errors; bounded context steepens decay (−0.69 vs −0.44); tool-result clearing "safest" compaction; ~1M → 86K in ~1 s anecdote. — [arXiv 2509.09677](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09677); [arXiv 2609.01660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01660); [Anthropic context](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents); [dsebastien](https://www.dsebastien.net/fast-jev-compaction/)
- Model/effort routing: RouteLLM 95% quality at 14–26% strong-model calls; ReDAct 15% deferral; LangChain router once per run. — [LMSYS](https://lmsys.org/blog/2024-07-01-routellm/); [ReDAct](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07036); [LangChain TypeSafe docs](https://docs.langchain.com/oss/python/integrations/providers/typesafe.md)
- Skill/tool selection: 95% top-1 on 76 labelled requests; 16.8% → 7.3% wrong-skill on 488 (self-reported); tool description rewrite −40% completion time. — [Hermes docs](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/plugins/jev-skill-router); [Anthropic multi-agent](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- Speculative fan-out of next action: 25% faster, 1,092 → 101 browser calls, no measured accuracy change. — [jev-ultrafast README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/browser-use/jev-ultrafast/main/README.md)
- Tool-output screening: Claude Code strips tool results from the gate and runs a separate injection probe; JevOut 61–73% flip rate shows the risk of judging on untrusted text. — [Anthropic auto mode](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-auto-mode); [arXiv 2609.30243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30243)

### Inferences
1. **Pre-execution gate on state-mutating / irreversible tool calls** (highest leverage). Mutating steps carry nearly all compounding risk (SABER), the model will not reliably pause on its own (GateArena 38–48% skip), and a learned gate can reach 0.4% FP on real traffic (Anthropic). A sub-second judgment here replaces a human prompt on the ~93% of actions users approve anyway, with threshold by reversibility class (≈0.7 read-only, ≥0.9 destructive) and deterministic floors above and below. Fail closed.
2. **Independent verifier over candidate outputs / trajectories at milestones** (verify-before-commit, best-of-N selection). Largest measured absolute gains (+8 to +13 points) at ~1/10 policy cost; most tractable when the judge is separate from the generator and is fed ground-truth signals (tests, DB state, browser). A cheap calibrated judge is the right tool for "which of these N is best" and "does this pass the sprint contract"; it is the wrong tool for open-ended critique.
3. **Continue / retry / replan / stop / escalate after each step** (loop-control decision). Termination errors are ~18% of MAST failures and step repetition another 17%; a calibrated "is the task done / is this going in circles / should a human or bigger model take over" judgment is cheap per step and is what Claude Code hard-codes with 3/20 denial counters. Evidence for the exact gains is thinner than for 1 and 2; calibration must be per agent.
4. **Per-item context keep / truncate / drop at compaction and after each tool result** (relevance-and-harm scoring). Supported by self-conditioning and context-rot evidence; the Jev plugin proves feasibility at ~1 s over ~1M tokens; quality unmeasured. Must see tool outputs, and must "pin" safety-relevant user statements.
5. **Per-step model/effort routing** (cheap model by default, defer on uncertainty). Best cost/quality curves in the literature (RouteLLM, ReDAct); agent-specific evidence is one paper; existing middleware routes once per run, so per-step routing is open ground.
6. **Skill / tool / subagent selection and abstention** (should a skill load; which tool; whether to delegate). Measured halving of wrong-skill loads (self-reported) and the lesson that losses come from the gate (abstention), not the choice.
7. **Speculative fan-out of candidate next actions** (decide operation + all plausible targets in one round trip). Measured only as latency and call-count reduction; accuracy benefit unproven; cheapest form of redundancy.
8. **Screening tool outputs and inter-agent messages for injection or irrelevance before they enter context** (what Claude Code does with a separate probe and SendMessage review). Important for safety but a decision model that reads the untrusted text is itself a JevOut target; best done on structured features or with a slower second stage.

### Gaps

    How this world works

    About one span in five is hard: a piece laid there is sound 65% of the time instead of 88%, and the inspector reads it less clearly. The inspector is a seeded toy judge that states an honest probability from one noisy load test. It is right most of the time and sometimes passes a flaw.

    The ratchet is the engine's chain runner. A piece is committed only when the inspection gate acts on it. Anything else is torn down and laid again, until the span's attempts or the shared budget run out; then the chain stops with the bridge unfinished and nobody on it. A flaw the inspector passed is the one way the ratchet still collapses.

    What to look for: the bar trades collapses against bridges that stop short, a shared budget turns stops into crossings, and a second crew with its own instrument cuts the flaws that get through without raising the bar.
